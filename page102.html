Napoli-Lazio, tifosi ospiti verso lo stop: atteso il decreto della Prefettura

📅 2026-11-04  |  Read Source →

Il divieto dovrebbe riguardare anche gli altri settori del Maradona per chi risiede nella Regione Lazio.

Il settore ospiti del Maradona dovrebbe restare chiuso per Napoli-Lazio, in programma domenica 8 novembre alle 12:30. Secondo quanto riportato da Radio Laziale, la trasferta sarà vietata ai sostenitori biancocelesti. La decisione non è ancora ufficiale: si attende il decreto della Prefettura di Napoli, previsto tra oggi e domani.

Il provvedimento dovrebbe impedire ai residenti nella Regione Lazio di acquistare biglietti per la partita. La restrizione non riguarderebbe soltanto il settore ospiti, ma anche gli altri settori dello stadio Diego Armando Maradona. Per i tifosi la notizia arriva dopo settimane segnate da una forte partecipazione alle trasferte della squadra.

Nei giorni scorsi l’Osservatorio Nazionale sulle Manifestazioni Sportive (ONMS) aveva rinviato la valutazione della gara al Comitato di Analisi per la Sicurezza delle Manifestazioni Sportive (CASMS). L’Osservatorio aveva inoltre invitato a non avviare la vendita dei biglietti destinati ai sostenitori ospiti.

Il quadro, secondo le indiscrezioni, sarebbe ora orientato verso la conferma del divieto e la chiusura del settore riservato ai tifosi della Lazio. Resta da attendere il decreto della Prefettura di Napoli: sarà il provvedimento ufficiale a formalizzare le restrizioni e a definirne le modalità di applicazione.

Se l’orientamento sarà confermato, la Lazio affronterà la trasferta senza i propri sostenitori provenienti dal territorio laziale. La limitazione all’acquisto dei biglietti, così come prospettata, si estenderebbe anche ai posti fuori dal settore ospiti.

Il possibile stop arriverebbe dopo una serie di partite lontano dall’Olimpico seguite da migliaia di sostenitori biancocelesti. A Reggio Emilia, in particolare, circa 10mila tifosi della Lazio hanno accompagnato la squadra, con una presenza significativa sugli spalti.

La partecipazione delle ultime settimane aveva alimentato le aspettative anche per la sfida al Maradona. Per i sostenitori, la partita con il Napoli rappresentava un appuntamento sentito sia per il valore della gara sia per la volontà di continuare a seguire la formazione biancoceleste in trasferta.

Il divieto interromperebbe questa sequenza di trasferte molto partecipate. Dopo l’esodo di Reggio Emilia, i tifosi residenti nel Lazio rischiano dunque di non poter assistere alla partita di Napoli, neppure acquistando un posto in un settore diverso da quello ospiti.

Time to admit Newcastle United have made a huge mistake, no not that one

📅 2026-11-04  |  Read Source →

Is it time to admit that Newcastle United have made a huge mistake?

“Any man can make mistakes, but only an idiot persists in his error” – Marcus Tullius Cicero

After another shocker of a Newcastle United performance against Everton last night, there is a growing anger and frustration in the fanbase with the direction (or lack of) of the team on field.

Consistent underperformers, no clear strategy, and players proven not to be good enough are selected regardless, though to be fair, managing to be toothless up top as well as all over at the back is impressive.

The knives are sharpening for Matthias Jaissle and he’s looking to be all mouth and no trousers. But for me, appointing Jaissle, isn’t the biggest mistake we’ve made in the last year or so, that dubious honour goes to the appointment of Ross Wilson (pictured at the top with David Hopkinson).

You might think and say well Wilson actually had a pretty decent CV, on paper it looks good, he was involved at Southampton when they brough Van Dijk in, was at Rangers when they broke Celtic’s grip on the SPL and was at Forest when they stayed up and then qualified for Europe.

Delve a bit deeper though and Rangers fans tend to give credit to his predecessor and are still trying to get rid of some of his flops and elsewhere while he’s had successes his overall recruitment strategy is scattergun at best. Theres no clear player type of strategy picked up, very opportunistic and he’s got good players at good prices (particularly getting Anderson from us) but it seems the strategy is slinging a lot at the wall and seeing what sticks.

If you look back since the takeover we’ve had two highly thought of Directors of football come in and go not long afterwards due to ‘issues’ with others at the club, so despite having a reasonable CV he didn’t come in with as big a reputation and to this observer his primary attribute was he was affable to Eddie and his nephew, which at the time made some sense as Eddie had put together an all time season in 24/25 and looked like he’d be our manager for years to come even after that summer of 25.

So Ross Wilson came in after that summer, which now with the benefit of hindsight has been nothing short of disastrous, with Elanga, Wissa and Ramsey being total overpays (as well as two of them debatably being our biggest wastes of money ever) and Woltemade being a glass slipper when we needed a steel toe capped boot.

Of course, we can’t put any of that on Wilson as he didn’t join till afterwards and it would be a challenge for even the best directors of football to deal with the fallout of it all.

The first warning signs for me came in the January 26 transfer window, when the team was on the ropes, Eddie was struggling and we didn’t bring in any first team recruits to assist a struggling squad. Now I know some of you will bring up the likes of PSR, FFP, SKY 6, Ashley, the Illuminati as reasons why we didn’t spend, but even one signing could have reinvigorated the squad and at least limited the late season slide we went on.

The risk is we’d have to sell at the end of the season to cover ourselves but we had to sell in any case because we plummeted down the league and didn’t even get any European football in one of the weakest Premier Leagues I can remember. It showed very little foresight for Ross Wilson to sit on his hands when the team was visibly on the ropes.

We then come to the end of the season where we have plummeted, put together some Bruce level performances and lost another derby which allowed our dear rivals to experience Europe for the first time in a lifetime. The performances were so poor and we looked totally bereft of ideas. Eddie Howe looked a shell of the man who’d once had us putting it up anyone we came up against.

The decision was there to be made, all logic pointed to a departure of a club legend but as Eddie Howe didn’t quit, Ross Wilson didn’t make the decision to let him go. There’s always the loyalty argument but at that stage it was just ignoring the evidence your eyes presented to you to keep him.

This eventually backfired as by mid pre-season Eddie took the decision out of his hands and left for some well-earned rest and relaxation. This put us further on the back foot as a lot of managers had already moved at the end of the previous season and we’d already started our season’s preparations expecting Eddie Howe to be our manager.

The next managerial move was to bring in Matthias Jaissle, who talked one hell of a game, got to give him and the club credit as the way he was presented looked amazing and had most of us very excited for the potential but then the football started. The ‘vertical football’ promised turned out to be an Allardyce like hoofball without the defensive solidity and if they’d played Stelios as their CF rather than Davies.

As a toon fan Ive had plenty of opportunity to make judgement on God awful managers and all I can see with Jaissle is another one.

Could Wilson have been duped like the rest of us? Of course he could of, but even if he was his actions with 50m+ in the bank to go and sign a left winger who thinks they are a forward when we needed a proper physical CF, a 10, pacy ball playing CBs and an inverted RW was just negligent. Don’t get me wrong, Matias Fernandez-Pardo could turn out great, but it was such a bad use of resources.

In a similar note the signing of Ewen Jaouen is looking like excess which wasn’t needed, again he could be great but spending eight figures on a back-up goalkeeper is just wasteful at best. Signing a bunch of youngsters for a manager who won’t give them minutes in general is foolish.

Overall, regardless of the many flaws Matthias Jaissle has shown, Ross Wilson hasn’t built a squad that’s fit for purpose for the system that Wilson hired the new Head Coach for.

Looking at what we did on outgoings we can also see issues with the way Wilson worked, our pet media liked to say we are trying to use the Brighton model, ignoring that the Brighton model is to bring in younger players who have time to settle in before selling your bigger names. This year we sold all three of Bruno, Gordon, and Tonali before bringing in replacements, putting us straight on the back foot.

We have different opinions on the value we got for those three players (personally I think we sold too early) but having a replacement ready for none of them just shows Ross Wilson hasn’t prepared properly.

Looking at other decisions made over the summer, giving Harvey Barnes an extension rather than cashing in seems similar to previous decisions not to cash in on the likes of Miggy/Wilson after they’d had hot streaks. Barnes puts up great goals and assists stats, and you’ll get the argument ‘where would we be without his goals,’ but I disagree and think a better all-round player will benefit the team more eventually.

The Palace game is the prime example of what Barnes is, great finish for his goal but the number of attacks of ours that broke down because of his unwillingness/inability to beat a man, poor passing and poor decisions was in double figures. Take that, Lanark Youth.

It would be massively unfair too and disrespectful in fact to dismiss McCann as a pundit turned manager, given his vast experience in the game as a hugely talented player and his previous managerial stints at Dundee, Inverness on an interim basis, as well as his time at Rangers as part of Barry Ferguson’s backroom team.

However, as McCann himself will know having been part of the Scottish football media set, his performance as manager of Kilmarnock will justifiably be subject to the same sort of scrutiny he used to apply to others, and at the risk of drawing a similarly icy stare to the one he fixed on former BBC colleague Chick Young at the weekend when he asked if McCann was feeling the pressure, any objective analysis would have to conclude that things are not going so peachily at present.

Killie currently sit in 11th place in the table, a point ahead of Hibs, who have a game in hand. Most concerningly, they are shipping goals for fun, conceding 2.3 per game so far this term, comfortably the worst record in the division. They have lost eight goals more than anyone else, letting in 28 across their 12 games to Motherwell and St Mirren’s 20. Their goal difference of -15 puts them seven worse off than the closest side, the Saints again, in that column.

McCann has been round the block often enough to know that the team who ships the most goals over the course of a campaign usually goes down. Never has the team with the worst defence in the league finished higher than 10th, which has also happened just twice.

Surely, stumbling to safety, whether that be through the playoffs or by scraping into tenth place, isn’t the limit of Kilmarnock’s ambitions this term? If they continue on their current trajectory, though, and carry on defending so poorly, then not only will that be their ceiling, it also seems a bit of a reach. It is fair to say then that the many sceptics who gave his appointment in the winter window a frosty reception were mainly won over by the fine job he did in the remainder of the campaign.

There was a great deal of optimism around in the summer, in fact, that having had the opportunity to build a squad in his image, that the issues such as the leaky defence that had plagued Killie would be ironed out, and all the while, he would retain the front-footed approach that had been so refreshing during his first months in charge.

The balance, though, is still way off kilter. While Killie still look decent going forward at times and play some nice stuff, there is no way of dressing up their persistent issues at the back. Their defending at times is absolutely criminal, and you can't be in a position where you need to score three times in a game just to have a chance of winning.

McCann held his hands up to a naïve approach when Celtic came and plundered four first half goals in an eventual 5-1 win at Rugby Park back in August, but naïve barely covers the way that his team twice gave up a lead against 10-man St Johnstone on Saturday.

This time, one of his own making.

In the end, it won’t be his erstwhile colleagues in the media that seal his fate, but those paying – and increasingly, braying – punters. If he doesn’t want to be back at Pacific Quay any time soon, he has to find a way to bolt the back door.

There are no guarantees of course that changing manager mid-season would help pull Kilmarnock out of this death spiral they are currently in. Though, admittedly, it did work for them the last time they tried it.

Time is surely running out for McCann to avoid being on the other side of that scenario.

Under-21s Beat Tamworth 3-2 In Entertaining Cup Clash

📅 2026-11-04  |  Read Source →

Everton Under-21s won their final National League Cup game at Tamworth on Tuesday evening, edging out the hosts by the odd goal in a 3-2 victory.

The tie was effectively a dead rubber as neither side could qualify from the group stage, so all credit to both sets of players for providing an entertaining contest.

Ramell Carter had a couple of early efforts for Tamworth. First, he cut inside from the right wing and tested Fraser Barnsley from outside the box, the Blues keeper making a good stop, before poking another effort wide on 10 minutes.

At the other end, Braiden Graham did well to control a bouncing ball on the artificial surface, but his eventual effort was off-target.

The 18th-minute opening Everton goal was a cracker. Harvey Foster guided the ball into the path of George Finney on the right and the full-back gave the home keeper Rhys Lovett absolutely no chance with a blistering drive from the edge of the box.

The response took six minutes and, unsurprisingly, it came from Carter who once again created space for himself from a wide area and this time Barnsley could not keep it out.

The advantage was restored in the 33rd minute and it was another nice goal. Graham dribbled into the penalty area and teed up Francis Gomez with an opening that he calmly accepted, slotting home from close range.

There was very nearly another instant reply, but Barnsley saved well to deny Oliver Dyson and then just before half-time Daniel Creaney hacked wildly over from close-range.

The first attempt of the second half came from Graham, but he didn’t generate sufficient power from 18 yards to really test Lovett.

Carter, Tamworth’s teenage loanee from Hull City, certainly did shortly after when he smashed his second goal of the night past Barnsley to restore parity.

On 57, a superb throw out from Barnsley released Ceiran Loney on the right and the big striker slid a neat pass into the path of Omari Benjamin who forced a fine save from the keeper.

But it was not long before Everton went back in front when Graham’s effort clipped a Tamworth defender and looped over Lovett into the net on the hour.

The Blues were indebted to Barnsley in the 73rd minute when he made a splendid diving save to keep out a Daniel Creaney header and a couple of minutes later he did even better to deny TJ Bramble after Everton had surrendered possession cheaply in a dangerous area.

Next to be frustrated by the excellent keeper was Hutchinson, whose effort was arrowing towards the top corner before Barnsley pushed the ball over the crossbar.

Graham came within a fraction of a fourth Everton goal when he pulled an angled shot narrowly wide with five minutes to go and then in added time he broke clear and fed the ball to Harvey Foster who could only fire his effort high over the bar.

Head of Academy Player Development Dave Hughes told evertonfc.com: "It was very entertaining and we tried to sell it to the players around the fact that we’ve given a good account of ourselves in the tournament, with the probable exception of Solihull.

"The guys were saying that they came (to Tamworth) last season and didn’t perform anywhere near the level they should have done so our message was to prove to people that you’re progressing and evolving and that last year was just an error. So we’re really pleased with them.

"Some of the younger guys came on and experienced a different type of football – Lewis Evans, Amari Moses, Kai Hutchison – loads of learning for them.

"It was physical but that’s what the game looks like. Some of our players who are out on loan are experiencing that. Everything goes into the box, every set-play or long throw goes in.

"Fraser Barnsley was excellent in goal and made three unbelievable saves and set off a couple of counter-attacks with positive distribution.

"We've finished third in the group and we’ve been super competitive against the men’s teams that we’ve played."
