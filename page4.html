3 Ways To Stop Your Kid From Being A Walking Petri Dish, Per A Pediatrician

📅 2026-11-10  |  Read Source →

You can’t germ-proof your kid, but a pediatrician says these three habits get you pretty close.

Most parents can relate to the joyous feeling of their child(ren) finally returning to school after summer break and, by mid-fall, (hopefully) hitting that back-to-school-routine stride. And conversely, the underlying anxiety of knowing it’s only a matter of time before they get sick and have to stay home. It’s a question worth asking: Is there anything we can do to prevent our kids from being a petri dish at school? I spoke with Dr. Brittany Odom, a board-certified pediatrician, mom, and trusted pediatric health educator, to see if she had any useful advice on preventing the many, many illnesses our children pick up this time of year.

“There are definitely some things we can do to prevent our kids from getting sick, but it is impossible to totally prevent it. So, them getting sick is not a parenting fail,” Dr. Odom wants to first point out. She then highlighted three ways we can take action against these seasonal illnesses.

A landing routine is what your kids do the moment they walk into your house after school. Kind of like stop, drop, and roll, minus the roll. Have them immediately remove any items from school, like their jackets, backpacks, and shoes.

Then, kids should wash their hands. “That way, we are not tracking all of the germs from school throughout the house,” she explains. Furthermore, this is even more important for families with babies at home because “older siblings bringing illnesses home is one of the most common ways babies get sick.”

“Your eyes, nose, and mouth create your T-zone, and this area is where a lot of germs can enter our bodies,” shares Odom. So simply emphasizing to our children to keep their hands away from this area is majorly helpful. Furthermore, if kids are apt to touch the T-zone regardless of what you say, enforcing handwashing is obvious, but worth stating again. “Washing hands at the most important times is helpful too. So, before we eat, after we use the bathroom, and before touching a baby sibling.”

If we can also teach kids to cough and sneeze into their elbow instead of their hands, that’s one more way to prevent the germs from entering the T-zone.

Beyond these three tips, Odom recommends that all children ages 6 months and older get both the flu and COVID vaccine. (Mom tip: An easy option for accomplishing this is CVS’s MinuteClinic, which is faster and oftentimes more convenient than a trip to the doctor’s office.)

Additionally, if you have an infant under 8 months old entering their first RSV season, and you did not receive the maternal RSV vaccine during pregnancy, talk to your pediatrician about getting this one too. It's also approved for babies 8 months to 19 months old who are considered "high risk.”

“Send your kids back when they are fever-free for at least 24 hours and symptoms have dramatically improved,” says Odom. As much as we want them out the next day, sending them back sick is harmful and disruptive to other students. And you don’t want to be that mom!

Since typical cold care medicines aren’t recommended for children under 4 years old, Odom says to focus on “supportive care.” These are ways to bring them some comfort and relief from their symptoms. Non-medicated saline sprays are useful, as well as a nasal aspirator. “There’s a lot of watchful waiting, making sure they are feeding well, still giving you wet diapers, and staying hydrated,” shares Odom. A humidifier is also helpful: “I recommend a cool-mist rather than warm-mist humidifier. It should be emptied and cleaned regularly to prevent mold or bacterial growth.”

Taking Odom’s words to heart, I tried to institute a landing routine today when my 3-year-old returned home from preschool. Sure, he said “no” when I asked him to wash his hands. But then, with the promise of some lollipops, he reconsidered. Hands washed, he immediately ran to my 6-month-old in her bouncer. And yes, he went for her T-zone, but hey, at least his hands were clean. I’ll take the win!

The Monaco summit that will decide the future of Carrick and Man Utd

📅 2026-11-10  |  Read Source →

Manchester United minority owner Sir Jim Ratcliffe (left), chief executive Omar Berrada (centre) and director of football Jason Wilcox will decide Michael Carrick's fate as head coach

Michael Carrick's future as Manchester United head coach will be determined 800 miles away from Old Trafford in Monaco on Wednesday.

Chief executive Omar Berrada and director of football Jason Wilcox are flying out for one of their regular pre-planned meetings to discuss strategy around the football club.

On this occasion, the main issue to be decided is Carrick's future and what should happen next.

It is expected that Berrada and Wilcox's recommendation is for the head coach to go.

It is not a situation Sir Jim Ratcliffe can have expected to find himself in again, less than three years after purchasing a minority stake in United.

On Christmas Eve 2023, his vision was much different.

"Our shared ambition is clear: we all want to see Manchester United back where we belong, at the very top of English, European and world football," he said.

Instead, United are 18th, in the Premier League's relegation zone after winning just one of their first 10 games and none since August. It is their lowest position so far into a top-flight season since 1986-87 when they were 20th.

Ratcliffe has been criticised for many decisions since he bought his 28.94% stake for just over Â£1bn. This is one he really needs to get right.

Ratcliffe's home is in Monaco and his company Ineos has offices in the principality.

The meeting had been in the diary for some time as a regular catch-up between senior executives and their boss.

Sources have stressed that the choice of location was not influenced by the debate around Carrick's future - it just happens to now be the most important item for discussion.

The decision to sack United's previous two managers was not made in a meeting in Monaco.

Ruben Amorim was sacked 19 hours after his explosive media conference following a 1-1 draw at Leeds in January.

Erik ten Hag went a day after Manchester United lost at West Ham leaving them with just three wins from their opening nine matches

A decision to sack Dutchman Ten Hag was taken at a meeting of senior Ineos executives in the summer of 2024, only for the choice to be reversed following the FA Cup final triumph over Manchester City.

Four months later there was a six-hour meeting at Ineos HQ in London as Ten Hag's tenure hung by a thread. He survived again but was gone three weeks later.

Amorim was summoned to Monaco after the Europa League final defeat to Tottenham in 2025.

There is daily contact between senior executives so if the meeting was purely to decide Carrick's fate, it could be done by video conference and the need to travel to Monaco is not the reason why there is yet to be a decision.

The travel element does add a complication given the preference for Berrada and Wilcox is to inform Carrick of any decision face to face.

Sources say the Monaco meetings used to be held every six weeks, although in more recent times they are less frequent.

Those who have attended them say they take place in the morning, tend to last between three and four hours and usually stop on time so Ratcliffe can go for his regular lunchtime jog.

United stood by Carrick after the woeful 3-0 home defeat by Bournemouth on 25 October.

Club sources were adamant they wanted to give Carrick and his players a chance to turn the situation around it was also felt that the two-week international break in November represented a better chance for the club to properly assess the club's start to the campaign.

However, although they did end a long wait for a victory against Roma on 3 November, United had to come from two goals down to seal a 3-2 victory in a chaotic game.

They failed to press home their advantage after excelling in the first half against Chelsea and eventually lost 2-0 as experienced central defender Lisandro Martinez was sent off.

On Sunday, they conceded twice inside five minutes and had to come from two goals down twice to claim a point against Villa.

While sources said the comebacks showed the United squad were still playing for Carrick, it is also understood the chaotic nature of the games and the inability to stop the opposition from scoring has led Berrada and Wilcox, who were at all three matches, to conclude a change of coach is required to get United out of the mess they find themselves in.

United have conceded 30 goals so far this season in 15 games - 19 in 10 Premier League matches, eight in four in the Champions League and three in their one Carabao Cup tie.

Instead of replacing Carrick another option under consideration is additional coaching support to assist the current team.

Bayern Munich coach Aaron Danks, who worked with Carrick at Middlesbrough and was touted as a potential appointment in the summer, is one option.

If Ratcliffe was to decide otherwise, it would raise serious questions over the future of Berrada and Wilcox.

How could they work with Carrick again in that scenario?

The pair have been the subject of intense and frequent fan discontent. But sources are adamant that Ratcliffe still retains faith in the pair, even though United are heading towards sacking a manager in mid-season for the third successive year, having backed them in the summer and spend millions on new players.

Ratcliffe is a ruthless operator, and has clear opinions of his own. He also has form, spending Â£5m hiring Dan Ashworth as sporting director from Newcastle in 2024, then waiting for the 55-year-old to spend five months on gardening leave before he could start work.

Five months later, Ashworth was sacked due to a divergence of opinion over who should replace Ten Hag. Ashworth felt someone with Premier League experience was a better fit for United's squad. Ratcliffe wanted a more modern alternative. Berrada suggested Amorim. Wilcox, after initial reservations, also got on board. Amorim was dismissed after 14 months.

The other unknown element is the Glazer family. They will need to sign off any decision.

At the time of the Ratcliffe purchase, United confirmed the club's 'board' had handed over responsibility for the football side. But United's articles of association state that responsibilities delegated by directors can also be "revoked or altered".

Crucially, of the 12 current directors, six are members of the Glazer family, including co-chairs Joel and Avi Glazer. United's rules state that if required, the chairman of any meeting will have a second and casting vote.

Although there have been media reports of Glazer dissatisfaction at United's current plight, there has been no public statement on the matter.

It may seem strange, but the reality is, as with any employee, Carrick will continue to do his job until told otherwise - and so far, there has been no conversation to that effect.

Although most of his squad, and a significant number of academy players, are on international duty, a core remains at home.

This includes Mason Mount, Joshua Zirkzee and Matthijs de Ligt. Harry Maguire, who suffered a back spasm against Villa, is also there, as are other injured players, including Luke Shaw and Marcus Rashford.

Carrick gave his players a day off on Monday but scheduled training days for Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday. After that, they are not due to report back until 17 November, by which time the international contingent should start to return and begin preparations for the trip to Liverpool on 22 November.

"Obviously some of the boys will be flying off around the world and different things, and the group will be split for 10 days or so, but as a normal international week is, we'll have some training and we'll have one or two days off," Carrick said after the Villa game. "Not too much. But we'll train and get ready for the next game."

NASA to Welcome Expedition 74 Astronauts Home at Space Center Houston

📅 2026-11-10  |  Read Source →

Johnson Space Center Office of Communications

NASA will welcome home the Expedition 74 crew during a public event at Space Center Houston following their return from a long-duration mission aboard the International Space Station.

NASA astronauts Jessica Meir, Jack Hathaway, and Chris Williams; ESA (European Space Agency) astronaut Sophie Adenot; and Roscosmos cosmonauts Sergey Kud‑Sverchkov and Sergei Mikaev will share mission highlights at 6 p.m. CST Tuesday, Nov. 17, at NASA Johnson Space Center’s visitor center. An awards ceremony recognizing key mission contributors will follow the presentation.

The astronauts will be available for media interviews before the event. Media may request an in-person interview no later than 5 p.m. Monday, Nov. 16, by emailing the JSC Newsroom at jsc-newsroom@mail.nasa.gov.

Williams, Kud‑Sverchkov, and Mikaev launched aboard the Soyuz MS-28 spacecraft from the Baikonur Cosmodrome in Kazakhstan on Nov. 27, 2025. The trio returned to Earth on July 26, making a parachute-assisted landing southeast of Dzhezkazgan, Kazakhstan. The crew was in space for 241 days, orbiting the Earth 3,856 times and traveling more than 102 million miles. The mission was the first for Williams and Mikaev and the second for Kud-Sverchkov.

Meir, Hathaway, and Adenot launched aboard a Dragon spacecraft on Feb. 13 as part of NASA’s SpaceX Crew-12 mission from Space Launch Complex 40 at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station in Florida. After more than seven months aboard the space station, Meir, Hathaway, and Adenot safely splashed down on Oct. 8 in the Pacific Ocean off the coast of Los Angeles.

During their 237-day mission, the four crew members traveled more than 100 million miles and completed more than 3,792 orbits around Earth. The mission was the second for Meir and the first for Hathaway and Adenot. Roscosmos cosmonaut Andrey Fedyaev also flew as part of Crew-12 but is unavailable for the event.

The Expedition 74 crew dedicated more than 1,400 hours to scientific research and technology demonstrations aboard the International Space Station. Its work advanced in-space manufacturing by testing metal and tissue 3D-printing technologies and supported human health studies examining the effects of long-duration spaceflight. Research included blood flow studies, assessments of piloting capabilities under changing gravitational conditions, and activation of a new compact exercise system. The crew also evaluated life-support and environmental systems critical to future exploration missions under NASA’s Artemis program.

In addition, the crew members conducted pharmaceutical research to crystalize proteins for cancer medications, advance tumor targeting treatments, and grow stem cells for therapeutics. They cared for the first set of plants studied outside of curated systems, upgraded an instrument used to advance our understanding of matter and develop new quantum technologies, managed visiting spacecraft, and performed essential maintenance to support the continued operation of the orbiting laboratory.

Beyond research and operational achievements, Expedition 74 engaged students and audiences around the world through live educational events, outreach activities, and the sharing of stunning imagery that highlights the importance of scientific discovery and international collaboration in space.

For more than 26 years, people have lived and worked continuously aboard the International Space Station, advancing scientific knowledge and making research breakthroughs not possible on Earth. The space station helps NASA understand and overcome the challenges of human spaceflight, expand commercial opportunities in low Earth orbit, and build on the foundation for long-duration missions to the Moon, as part of the Artemis program, and to Mars.

NASA Invites Media, Public to Join Voyager 1 One-Light Day Activities

📅 2026-11-10  |  Read Source →

Launched nearly 50 years ago in 1977, NASA’s Voyager 1 on Wednesday, Nov. 18 will have traveled one light-day from Earth, which is more than 16 billion miles (25.9 billion kilometers). Voyager 1 is the most distant human-made object, and any light from Earth would take 24 hours to reach the spacecraft. NASA will highlight this historic milestone with two events.

On Tuesday, Nov. 17, NASA invites the world to participate in “Shine Bright for Voyager’s Light-Day” by creating a symbolic wave of golden light encircling the planet after sunset. NASA has created a website that enables smart phones and tablets to flash “VGR”, Voyager’s call sign, in morse code.

For more information about how to participate in “Shine Bright for Voyager Light-Day,” visit:

Everyone is encouraged to capture photos or videos of their light-day signal and share it on social platforms, tagging @NASA.

On Thursday, Nov. 19 at 12 p.m. EST (9 a.m. PST), NASA’s Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL) in Southern California will host a moderated discussion with mission members and NASA leadership about the status of Voyager 1 and 2, the unique science the twin probes continue to gather in interstellar space, and the challenges of keeping the spacecraft operating for so long over such vast distances. A Q&A with media and other audience members will follow. In addition, there also will be a preview of the upcoming NASA+ Voyager documentary, planned for release in 2027 for the mission’s 50th anniversary.

The event will take place in NASA JPL’s von Kármán Auditorium, where JPL hosted Voyager briefings during the spacecraft’s grand tour of the outer planets.

U.S. media interested in attending the event in person must RSVP by Monday, Nov. 16 to Josh Smooha at: josh.smooha@jpl.nasa.gov. To be considered for onsite credentials, foreign national media must RSVP no later than Thursday, Nov. 12. Media interested in participating virtually must RSVP no later than two hours before the event. NASA’s media accreditation policy is available online.

For almost half a century, the agency’s Voyager mission has blazed a trail through space. Voyager 1 and Voyager 2 were designed to explore the outer planets by taking advantage of a planetary alignment that occurs once every 176 years. Voyager 1 entered interstellar space in 2012. Following a different trajectory, Voyager 2 entered in 2018. Both spacecraft continue to provide unique scientific observations of the environment beyond the heliosphere, the protective bubble created by the Sun.

Managed by Caltech in Pasadena, California, NASA JPL built and operates the Voyager spacecraft. NASA’s Deep Space Network, also managed by NASA JPL, enables communication with the distant probes. The Voyager mission is funded by NASA’s Heliophysics Division at NASA Headquarters in Washington.
