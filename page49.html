'Forget Michael Carrick: These are the three people responsible for Man United's dreadful discipline'

📅 2026-11-07  |  Read Source →

Michael Carrick's bosses should be questioned on the slipping standards at Manchester United.

That is the opinion of experienced former Everton, Tottenham and Aston Villa scout, Bryan King, speaking exclusively to Football Insider.

The Red Devils finally put an end to their run of nine consecutive games without victory on Tuesday when they beat Roma 3-2, despite being forced to end the game with just 10 men.

Matheus Cunha, who scored twice to put Man United ahead, launched into a ridiculous and needless challenge in the final seconds, earning himself a second yellow card for no good reason.

It was the third red card for Michael Carrick's side this season, and the second within the space of just a few days.

Lisandro Martinez was sent off for a second yellow card against Chelsea last week, giving away a penalty in the process after catching Wesley Fofana.

MAN UNITED HAVE TURNED IT AROUND!!! 🔴🔥 From 2-0 down, to 3-2 up at Old Trafford 🤩 pic.twitter.com/ATD8wDlc1e

Though Carrick has bought himself some time with victory on Tuesday, he will likely be expected to secure three points against Villa on Sunday if he is to survive the international break.

He will hope to see his team get through 90 minutes without having a man sent off, if he is to prove successful.

"When I saw the game on television, they showed the close-up of Carrick and Steve Holland," King told Football Insider.

"And then they showed the three sitting up in the director's box, [Jason] Wilcox and the other two, Morecambe and Wise [Omar Berrada and Jim Ratcliffe]. These are the people who should be questioned here.

"These are the people who brought in these players. Look at their recruitment. Look at the discipline within the football club, sporting director, the director of football.

"These are the people who should be working together with Carrick and saying, 'right, I want these players brought into my office because the standard of discipline has dropped dramatically and we want to know why from these players.'"

While Man United's lack of discipline will be a concern for Carrick, the result is ultimately of more importance.

Should the Red Devils manage to beat Villa on Sunday, the Englishman could well have carved out a future at Old Trafford for himself.

"I was a bit disappointed with how it finished, but it just shows how much he cares and he’s willing to do when he’s in that frame of mind," Carrick said on Cunha's red card on Tuesday.

"When he’s in that mood, and he plays like that, that’s why we want him here.”

St Johnstone boss banishes Dundee demons with ‘bitter’ declaration

📅 2026-11-07  |  Read Source →

Simo Valakari was thrilled to give St Johnstone supporters a 2-1 victory over Dundee to celebrate after recent Perth struggles against their Tayside rivals.

Dundee won twice at McDiarmid Park in 2024-25, the season Saints were relegated, but a transformed team has returned to the top flight under Valakari.

They drew level on 17 points with Dundee in the William Hill Premiership thanks to a fourth win of the season. Valakari lapped up the success, with fans joyous at a first home victory over the Dark Blues since October 2021.

“We are a totally new team and it is not good to hold on to all those memories,” said Valakari. “Me as a manager, of course I remember. At Christmas-time we were 3-0 down after 20 minutes of the biggest game of the season.

“And of course I remember when their fans were on the pitch celebrating on the last day. But my players don’t have this baggage. We are creating new experiences together; this is our new adventure.

“If I carried everything that happened in the past with me, I would be a very bitter person! You need to let things go; that’s why we didn’t even bring it up.

“But all the players knew how much this game meant to our fans. We needed to perform, and we did. And to perform the way we did against a very good team made it a deserved win.”

Josh Campbell headed Saints in front after five minutes before substitute Joe Westley replied early in the second half following a mix-up between goalkeeper Ross Sinclair and Cheick Diabate. Kai Fotheringham, on his first Premiership start for Saints, netted a deflected winner in the 72nd minute.

“We were very good in the first half,” said Valakari. “They changed things at half-time. It took us a bit of time to adjust and we conceded a goal in that period.

“My team showed their character and personality again. We got control back and it got better and better from that.”

Dundee manager Steven Pressley conceded that his team failed to match much of their early-season form. He admitted St Johnstone were deserving winners over the course of the match.

“Over the course of the 90 minutes, St Johnstone were probably deserving of the victory,” he said. “We didn’t quite reach the heights and the standards that we’ve set.

“It’s not a criticism, it’s just these players continuously produced really high performances and have set some incredible standards. We were just a little bit below them on some of the things that we pride ourselves on.

“And, on the things we pride ourselves on, I thought St Johnstone were very strong. We’re disappointed to go into an international break on a defeat.”

Jim Goodwin bites tongue with ‘careful’ admission after VAR intervention

📅 2026-11-07  |  Read Source →

Boss Jim Goodwin hit out at the decision to award the penalty that cost Dundee United dear in a 1-1 draw against Motherwell at Tannadice.

Referee Ryan Lee had initially given a free-kick for offside in the early stages of the game.

However, he was then advised by VAR Andrew Dallas to have another look at an incident where Tawanda Maswanhise had gone down in the United box under a challenge by home skipper Dylan Tait.

After checking the pitchside monitor, the whistler pointed to the spot with Motherwell captain Lukas Fadinger tucking the penalty away.

The Terrors ultimately salvaged a point thanks to a Jesse Randall goal but Goodwin insisted his side should have had all three.

He said: “It’s a poor decision for me. I know it’s going to be subjective and people will have their own opinions on it, but I don’t know what Dylan Tait is meant to do.

“We all know Maswanhise runs at pace, he’s driving at the defender and I think he’s going down before the contact arrives.

“Dylan is pulling out and I just don’t understand what a defender is meant to do in that situation.

“The linesman initially was giving an offside but neither he or the ref thought there was a foul in that particular instance.

“We were happy with that but when VAR check it there’s not the offside and they decide to carry on the check into the penalty incident.

“I need to be careful what I say because I got in trouble a few years ago talking about players going over easily in the penalty area.

“But I think a bit of experience, knowing the type of players you’re dealing with, the referee at that moment made the right call.

“When the referee got called to the monitor, you have an experienced referee there basically telling a young ref he’s made a mistake.

“So it takes a bit of courage for the ref on the park to overrule that decision from the VAR room. It’s gone against us but we reacted positively.”

Not surprisingly, Motherwell manager Alfred Johansson disagreed with Goodwin’s assessment of the penalty decision and instead insisted it should have been awarded.

The 36-year-old said: “I haven’t watched it back with a close-up on the situation in the box yet but the way I saw it JT (Maswanhise) is running at high speed into the box. He’s on the ball and gets down from a tackle, and it’s a penalty.

“So it depends on who you ask. None of us can complain with the draw, it was a very tight game. It was very open but definitely fair with the draw.”

En route to their second NWSL championship in two years, relatively new Gotham FC recruit Jaedyn Shaw was uninterested in the narratives that underestimated her team. The surprise win at the NWSL Shield-winning Kansas City Current in the playoff quarterfinals was not a surprise to her, and she made it clear with three quick words: "Underdog, my ass."

Shaw had a point on that cold day in the Midwest, and almost exactly a year later, it still stands. Gotham are the NWSL's new dynasty, and their Shield win marked their fifth major title in three years, with a sixth in their sights as the playoffs begin this weekend. The transformation was rapid for a team that has historically been a bottom-dweller, so much so that their 2023 championship triumph was a genuine surprise. They quickly built a title-winning team and reaped the rewards just as swiftly, but even as the muscle memory of title charges returns, Gotham will go about their next NWSL playoff journey differently. The target is officially on their backs, the onus officially on them to live up to the billing.

Gotham have played the vast majority of their playoff games as the lowest-seeded team in the postseason, winning the championship twice in those circumstances. Sunday's quarterfinal against the Seattle Reign, though, will be the first game they play as the unquestionable favorites to win the whole thing. How Gotham will play with the weight of expectations is entirely unclear, since they have never done it before, but it is a good problem of their own creation, one they have chased since the 2023 triumph.

Their blitz in the free-agent market in the winter of 2024 laid the foundation for their dynastic run, but the most fascinating feature of Gotham's years-long run of success is the evolution they have undergone since. U.S. women's national team defender Tierna Davidson has become the team's captain, her progressive passing ability making her a key fixture in head coach Juan Carlos Amoros' system. Her club and country teammate Rose Lavelle has become a standout, but the star of the show has been Shaw, who has barely missed a beat since her arrival in September 2025.

Shaw has 14 goals and seven assists in 35 NWSL games with Gotham, her wide-ranging skill set fully on display in Amoros' team. She has been a little bit of everything for the team's attack, which is arguably too reliant on her talents, but it is almost hard to fault Amoros for the approach. The system has allowed Shaw to flourish, in particular bringing out a clear goal-scoring quality that is key to a Gotham team that values defensive work rate first.

"First, I think the commitment they have to defend and the commitment they have to work hard for the team despite being some of the best players technically in the planet," Amoros said about both Shaw and Lavelle after a win over the Chicago Stars in September. "I think that helps the connection, not only between them two. There is a mutual respect there because of that but then also from the whole team. When you have to play and those are the players that produce the chances for us in the final third but then the people that [are] behind them are always supportive of them because of the work they put in and then we try to work a lot with them to how we can find them and how they can connect more with each other and the same with Sam [Kerr]."

He has found a unique way to make Shaw and Lavelle work together, despite the fact that they appear to have overlapping skill sets when it comes time for USWNT head coach Emma Hayes to slot them into her lineup. The pair often play just behind Kerr, an out-and-out striker, and assume the role of strikers themselves while balancing their creative tendencies. They have filled in some gaps along the way -- Kerr, for example, ranks third in the league with 9.67 expected goals since returning to the NWSL on July 16 but has scored just four times. Kerr does, though, have four assists in 17 games, one fewer than Shaw and one more than Lavelle over that span.

The connection between Lavelle and Shaw has offered Gotham a level of dynamism that has been missing in their attack at times over the years, even as they racked up title after title. It helps that the two have enjoyed working alongside each other, the 21-year-old Shaw in particular taking inspiration from Lavelle, a decade her senior.

"I think her movement is some of the best in the world, honestly," Shaw said after that September win over the Stars. "She's able to make stretch runs, drag players. She attracts so many players, opening up space for other players to get on the ball and then getting on the second one, I think, is one of her strengths and is something I'm trying to replicate off of her. I think she's just incredibly smart in soccer knowledge."

For each of them, a return to fitness after small injuries in the final weeks of the regular season comes not a moment too soon. Kerr's inconsistency is not the only attacking issue they will need to overcome -- Jordyn Dudley will miss the postseason with a foot fracture she sustained last month, her impressive rookie season coming to a close with four goals and three assists. Lavelle and Shaw will be particularly useful against the Reign, whose defensive qualities continue to be a strength.

Shaw has become the focal point of Gotham's reinvention in recent years, but Amoros' side has added experienced options in a wide variety of positions since the 2023 championship. The vast majority of Gotham's likely starters on Sunday will have joined the club after the 2023 playoff run, a steady stream of important players signing themselves up since. Ann-Katrin Berger, the 2024 goalkeeper of the year, made the move not long after Davidson and Lavelle did, and European champion Jess Carter followed right after. Midfielder Jaelin Howell, who has been in and around the USWNT, was recruited in time for the 2025 season, and Shaw joined months later. Left back Guro Reiten has two goals and six assists since making her club debut in April, while Kerr made the move at roughly the same time as midfielder Denise O'Sullivan, a two-time NWSL champion with the North Carolina Courage.

Gotham's reinvention is constant, the core evolving each and every season. It is a unique route to the top, but in another season of change, they found the consistency they have been chasing since the 2023 championship. The underdog label has well and truly been shed, the team chasing another tag entirely -- a third championship would put them level with the Portland Thorns as the most successful team in the league's history.

LONDON -- Though there was some to-ing and fro-ing as to the order in which the offenses should be considered, it seemed fitting that what would have been Arsenal's best scoring chance of the first half of their 1-0 win over Hull City was wiped out for an offside. Here is a team whose attack has been drifting so out of sync that promising situations fall apart without the opponent needing to get involved.

Sometimes the opponent is even willing to offer them a helping hand. Only the members of the International Football Association Board might be able to say for sure whether Arsenal should have had a penalty when Kai Havertz's shirt was pulled by Hull City's Nobel Mendy as Bruno Guimaraes took a long-range shot. The striker was impeded, but he had been standing in an offside area. Was he attempting to play the ball when Mendy grabbed hold of his jersey? If not, then the foul should have taken precedence and Arsenal should have had a penalty. Instead, they had to wait until the 72nd minute for their win, Martin Odegaard bundling in the rebound off Noni Madueke's shot.

At least that meant Mikel Arteta could jokingly say he had not seen the spot kick that might have been. There may be a question about that particular penalty and offside, but the big-picture statistics are mind-boggling, such that there surely has to be some air in these numbers.

In the last seven Premier League seasons, no team has averaged more than three offsides per game. Only Burnley in 2021-22 and Liverpool two years later have averaged more than 2.5, in the process reaching over 100 for the season. In their 10th game of the season, Arsenal committed offside offenses Nos. 32, 33, 34 and 35. At the final whistle, that was 11 more than any other team in the league, and there is a very real prospect that lead does not get eaten into. Field a team of Pippo Inzaghis and you'd do well to put up these numbers.

There has to be something to this, right?

What makes this all the more curious is that Arsenal have not been a team that particularly struggled to stay onside before this season. In the previous six campaigns, they were caught about as often as the average Premier League team when you adjust for possession. When these relatively low-volume statistics warp like this, at least part of the answer is going to be variance. A bit more of it might be that Arsenal have a 10-game sample size that includes Aston Villa. Still, that sample is over a quarter of the season. Arsenal have had a string of games where they have been caught offside with frequency.

This looks like a problem. Even Arteta acknowledged that. "There are many areas that we need to improve. That's one, that's related sometimes to the timing or the situation, or really the position [of the opponent], where he's lined up and what space he's given you.

"Certainly it's far too many. It's true that the Villa game obviously he's putting those stats in a different perspective. I think the sample is quite short to analyse it, but you have a point there."

As for the Villa game, Mikel, yes, that's eight of your 35. Leeds is seven, though. Coventry five, Hull four. These are all well above the Premier League average. The chief culprit for this spate of infractions is certainly Havertz. His 15 offsides are not just seven more than any other player in the top flight; they are more than any Arsenal player committed in the entirety of last season's title campaign.

Again, this feels a little unprecedented. In the six seasons prior to this one, Havertz ranked 48th among forwards in the division for how frequently they were caught offside. The worst offenders, your Ashley Barneses, Darwin Nunezes and Cristiano Ronaldos, might get flagged once every 70 or 80 minutes. Havertz hovered around the 140 mark. This season it's not far off once per half.

Has he lost some burst? Gradient Sports' physical data would suggest he is moving about as swiftly this season as any other. A broader view is that this attack might have greater joy punishing high lines with more pacey options in it. For a player who makes so many runs off the ball, Madueke is caught offside relatively infrequently (though his was one of the four today). That is probably because he can give his man a yard in a way that Havertz or even Bukayo Saka, third in the league with seven offsides, can't quite.

Have defenses sussed Havertz's movement out? That doesn't seem entirely clear, not least given that Hull misjudged their line more than once, allowing Havertz in a one-on-one position where he should have done much better than cut the ball onto Mendy's trailing leg.

Arteta's explanation is simple. "It's all about timing," he said. "So there is a signal on the ball, and you need to time it around that. If that's a second too late or half a second too late, that's all."

Certainly Arsenal could release the ball a bit quicker. If there is a flaw in the game of Odegaard, whose nine passes to an offside teammate also lead the league, it is that he tends to want that one extra touch. That much was apparent in the win at Aston Villa, when the captain had the ideal chance to slip Havertz through in position to bend one in the far post. He took three touches when two would have sufficed, and it was all a moment too late.

Bruno Guimaraes (four such passes) is in a similar vein. And perhaps Arsenal as a club are a bit too much of that ilk. Does the specter of Alex Hleb and Samir Nasri still haunt this team? Arteta might have said as much, that the thing about Arsenal is they always try to walk it in.

"We need to find ways to be more efficient, sometimes even more pragmatic," he said. "Sometimes my feeling is that we are trying to score a perfect goal against these low blocks. We need to be much more chaotic than that."

You can see that in some of those offsides above, especially the smattering around the right flank that Arsenal like to make their creative hub. It's the attempted reverse balls, the quick one-twos and slide-rule passes to a teammate on the overlap. Offside No. 36 might well have been another of those, an overlapping run from Jurrien Timber where Madueke released the pass a beat too late and the Dutchman stopped rather than cede Hull a free kick.

Instead, Madueke proved the value of an alternative approach right throughout this match. Driving at his fullback time and time again, he manufactured positions where things might happen without having to worry about a give-and-go or timing his run correctly. In an age where so many opponents, including Hull, are more intent on gumming up their opposition than gritting and grinding, a bit of unpredictability from one of Arsenal's own players goes a long way. As for the offsides, of course that feels like a problem, a lot of possessions wasted by Arsenal players' own inability to get on the right beat. It has been. It does not mean it is going to be. Injuries mean that Havertz and Odegaard have rarely shared the pitch over the last few seasons. Get them and Saka together for weeks on end and the old rhythm will come back to them, and this should be nothing more than a weird, if intriguing, blip.
