Newcastle United Women’s team tear woeful Wearsiders apart

The Newcastle United Women’s team went into their latest League Cup game looking for their first win in the competition this season, with three defeats from three group games (of six in total) so far.

Manager Tanya Oxtoby selected a strong side to take on what was a pitiful Durham side at South Shields, as the Lasses collected their first points in the Subway Players League Cup.

The Newcastle United Women’s team scored five goals for the first time under Tanya Oxtoby as they tore the woeful Wearsiders apart.

However, despite the visitors dominating from kick-off Durham did take a shock lead on 14 minutes when a scramble in front of goal from Lucy Watson’s corner wasn’t cleared and United goalie Kayla Rendell was unable to keep it out at the near post with the ball somewhat fortuitously crept over the line.

Durham were buoyed by that goal and started to play more physically going forward, leaving themselves exposed at the back, which played perfectly into the hands of Newcastle who started to tear them apart.

Newcastle were effective at moving the ball quickly and nearly took the lead when top scorer Martha Thomas was played through before drawing two defenders with her. A clever back heel set up the on-rushing Beth Lumsden in the area but her strike lacked accuracy and sailed wide of the far post.

With the Lasses finding their groove and Durham left appealing for offside, Lumsden pounced and raced towards the byline, before setting up Kathleen McGovern from six yards out, but the Scottish international was inches away from making contact.

Their persistence finally paid off though soon after and they were level by the mid-point of the half. Thomas latched onto a defence-splitting pass from Mollie Lambert, racing through on goal, holding off Durham captain Sarah Wilson before guiding her effort into the far corner.

Durham’s goal scorer tried her luck again soon after as she shot from the edge of the box and forced Rendell into a low save, with the ball squibbing free and only just going around the post. Perhaps two slices of luck were too much to ask for by the home side.

Straight away Newcastle went back on the attack. From a quickly taken throw-in Thomas found Lumsden in space, with the flying winger able to run and cut the ball back to Jordan Nobbs who looked to have swept home only for the ball to narrowly drift wide.

But with less than ten minutes before half time Newcastle eventually grabbed their go-ahead goal. Nobbs was at the heart of it as she picked out Lumsden down the left flank who in turn cut inside onto her weaker foot and curled home in off the far post for 2-1.

Nobbs could have made it a two goal advantage just before the break as she was found between two defenders. But her cross could only be glanced on by Thomas who hit the underside of the bar.

Five minutes into the second half and the Lasses did have their third goal. Lumsden turned inside Grace Ede before sending in an inch perfect cross for Thomas who slipped past her marker and volleyed home from twelve yards out.

It soon became 4-1 when Nobbs found Lumsden on the edge of the box, before the latter allowed the ball to bounce and fired home with Durham’s goalie Anna King powerless to stop it.

The scoring was complete when a Katie Bradley corner was sliced by Jade Pennock into her own net.

A truly comprehensive victory against a side still looking for their first points in either competition this season. Although perhaps Durham can always say they have appeared on hit TV show Ted Lasso.

Newcastle United: Kayla Rendell, Charlotte Wardlaw, Gemma Evans, Aoife Mannion, Kaitlyn Torpey, Lois Joel, Mollie Lambert (Hildur Antonsdóttir 83), Jordan Nobbs (Katie Bradley 65), Beth Lumsden (Oona Sevenius 65), Kathleen McGovern (Jessie Gale 65), Martha Thomas (Freya Gregory 59).

Subs not used: Cat Sheppard, Maddy McComasky, Lucy Noble, Emma Brown

Where that leaves Newcastle United Women’s team

Despite Newcastle’s emphatic win, it’s still unlikely they will progress to the knockout stages of this cup competition.

They have two games remaining, albeit both at home, but one is against runaway leaders Manchester United.

Back to the important stuff and top of the second tier Newcastle return to League action on Sunday, 8th November with Burnley set to visit at St James’ Park.

It will be the first of seven scheduled games at SJP this season.

After six matches of a 22 game league season, the Newcastle United Women’s team are top on 16 points.

It is vital they end the season there, as only the team that finishes top will be guaranteed promotion to the Super League.

Why this season feels so uncertain as a Newcastle United fan

📅 2026-11-05  |  Read Source →

Hope you are all doing well, I am in my 40s and a long time Newcastle United fan living in Australia.

I eventually got the chance in September to come and see my first game at St James’ Park against Hull – it was brilliant.

I watch all the matches, read, listen to pods etc and have played all my life (I’m 42).

I’m also into the tactical side of things and have undertaken some of my coaching badges here in Australia.

I’m sharing my first submission to The Mag about why I think this season feels so uncertain as a Newcastle United fan.

Newcastle United are a club of ups and downs – we all know that. The club’s current reality feels like we’re on the way down, though in reality it is more like a straight line from last season than any sort of significant change :

2026/27 13th place in the Premier League after 9 games. 11 points in those 9 games at an average of 1.22 points per game. 2025/26 12th place in the Premier League after 38 games. 49 points in those 38 games at an average of 1.28 points per game.

There are lots of reasons that this season feels more ominous than last season, though primarily it’s because we have no clear idea what to expect from this manager and from this group and players. We don’t know what to expect.

For Newcastle United fans, this season is full of unknowns.

Was Eddie Howe keeping our heads above water?

Who on earth are half of these new players?

Could we… go down, or could we actually end up being alright with a few tweaks?

Never in recent years have Newcastle United fans had so much to be uncertain about.

Under the management of Eddie Howe, there was certainty. We knew him and he knew us. There was no chance we were getting relegated under Howe, even through last season’s struggles.

We knew he knew the league. We knew he knew how to win games in this league. We knew he knew the histories, we knew he knew just what a cold, wet night in Stoke meant. We knew he could (mostly) improve players on an individual basis and get the best out of them. We knew the players respected him, liked him and trusted him. We knew his tactics and his style, and we knew they worked (mostly).

We knew Howe’s team. We knew that when the chips were down, Bruno would take the lead. We knew he’d demand the ball, that he’d try with every last ounce of determination and skill to create something, to inspire his teammates and to inspire the fans. We knew big Joe had his back and would kick someone if he had to, or even if he didn’t. We knew we had a threat up front or out wide to hurt opponents. We knew we had guys who could score goals in the Premier League. We knew Tripps wouldn’t stand for a team that didn’t give their all. We knew we had high standards, kept by high quality players who had done it all and seen it all.

Now, all that safety and certainty is gone and you know what – it’s freaking us out. Without the guard-rails that the certainty of Howe provided for us, we’re contemplating the worst. We’re scared of free fall. We’re not cautiously optimistic – we’re venturing towards riotously pessimistic.

So, now, after a similar start to last season in terms of points, we’re worried. Not worried like we were last year – this time it’s different. We’re worried because we’ve got no idea what comes next. We’ve got no idea how Matthias Jaissle responds or what he might do to get a tune out of this team. We’ve got no real indication yet that this group of players can actually deliver at this level, under any manager.

We don’t know how PIF, David Hopkinson and Ross Wilson will respond to this, because they haven’t had to before. We don’t fully know whether they even know what they’re doing to be honest. They seem to for the most part, but here we are nine league games in, with fingernails dwindling, a hard run of games ahead and a bunch of youngsters or also rans to take us through to the derby and Christmas and the crazy winter schedule..,so maybe they don’t know what they’re doing?

So, while we gaze into the unknown, let’s also think about the knowns. We know we’ve got a great set of fans. We know that when St James’ Park is rocking we can get the team over the line against almost anyone. We know this year they might well need that and they’ll need us behind them to succeed in this league.

We know we’re in transition. We know we care. We know we want the team to succeed whoever the manager is, wherever he’s from and whoever is on the pitch. We know it’s going to be turbulent because when has it ever not been? And we also know, we don’t want to support anyone else.

A lot of focus on the eight Newcastle United signings that were brought in across summer 2026.

There were actually nine in total but teenager Ousmane Diabate was instantly loaned back to the selling club in Turkey in order to get regular first team football.

So what about these eight Newcastle United signings that have gone straight into the first team squad?

I have had a look at the minutes played so far this 2026-27 season by the new players.

Specifically, the minutes that each of the eight have played in the Premier League.

I think it tells a very clear story now we are nine Premier League matches (810 minutes) into this 2026-27 season.

Total Premier League minutes (out of a possible total of 810) played by the eight Newcastle United signings with breakdown match by match (Ev Everton, CP Crystal Palace, AV Aston Villa, Cov Coventry, H Hull, Le Leeds, B Bournemouth, T Tottenham, Li Liverpool):

0 Ewen Jaouen (Zero minutes in any of the nine Premier League matches)

115 Bazoumana Toure (3Ev, 34AV, 30Cov, 16H, 33Le)

256 Sean Steur (26Ev, 5CP, 66H, 71 Le, 13B, 19T, 56Li)

304 Matias Fernandez-Pardo (56AV, 90Cov, 74H, 57Le, 27B)

501 Nico Gonzalez (64Ev, 90CP, 90AV, 90Cov, 19Le, 77B, 71T)

536 Amar Dedic (87Ev, 90CP, 31Cov, 58Le, 90B, 90T, 90Li)

810 Lukas Hornicek (90Ev, 90CP, 90AV, 90Cov, 90H, 90Le, 90B, 90T, 90Li)

What stands out for me, is that Matthias Jaissle has given far more Premier League minutes to the (relatively) older and more experienced Newcastle United signings.

Three of the eight Newcastle United signings were aged 24 when they arrived, the other five were 21 or under.

Lukas Hornicek has played every Premier League minute this season.

Nico Gonzalez has started all seven EPL matches he has been available for.

Amar Dedic has started all of the EPL matches he was in a position to start, as well as off the bench against Coventry.

The other five Newcastle United signings who were aged 21 and under when they arrived at St James’ Park, have played 702 minutes between them. That is less than Lukas Hornicek alone has played and would also be less than the Dedic and Gonzalez individual totals if they had been available all of the time.

On the other hand, Matias Fernandez-Pardo has played far more minutes than I think he would have otherwise done so far, simply because of so many injured players in the early part of the season.

I am sure that Matthias Jaissle sees the exciting potential of Sean Steur just as much as we do, probably even more so considering he watches him every day in training. However, my belief is that the Head coach is simply trying to protect the 18-year-old from too much expectancy and pressure too soon. The same with the other younger players.

I don’t think he is holding the likes of Toure and Bamba back from playing more minutes so far because of any negative irrational reason, more a case of giving these younger inexperienced players from weaker leagues a chance to integrate and build up their confidence rather than throwing them in at the deep end and a case of sink or swim?

The booing from many Newcastle United fans at the Everton match for example, it isn’t exactly something that will help younger players to build confidence if they are subjected to that.

📅 2026-11-05  |  Read Source →

It all started with my dad who used to go to both Everton and Liverpool games well before my time.

Over the years, he started a family and, thankfully, his allegiances started to move towards Everton.

Jimmy, my eldest brother, came along years later and he started following the Blues straight away, going to the games as soon as he was allowed to.

I came along in 1984 and soon became the newest Blue in the house.

From the age of around 10, Jimmy took me to my first game at Goodison and I fell in love with Everton from that day. Me and Jimmy went to every home game and a few of the local away fixtures as well.

Our dad didn’t really go to many games with us. He did start coming again around 1999 but had a heart attack after one of the games. He never went again – but followed from the couch at home.

Moving forward some years Jimmy met his future wife, Christine, who at the time was a Leeds fan... not for long, though! It took only three games watching the Blues to convert her and extend our home seats by another.

We loved the tradition of a home game day: the meet-up at my mum and dad's house and the drive listening to Radio City’s football talk.

We had season tickets for many years until Christine became ill and needed constant treatment. We all decided to give them up and buy each game separately if and when we could around her treatment.

Unfortunately, after a year, Christine lost her battle and left us. Jimmy needed to get back to Goodison as soon as possible, so again, we all started going to every game together.

Skip through to 2024 and our dad passed away. Another blue light gone out.

Me and Jimmy made sure to remember him at the new stadium with a stone and photo inside.

Jimmy was obsessed with the new stadium build, watching drone progress daily. He waited so long for a new stadium. Unfortunately, Jimmy also passed away just two months after our dad, not getting to step inside the new stadium he always dreamed of.

Again, with losing my dad and Jimmy, who I always went the game with, I decided that I would also stop going.

For the past two seasons I've supported from home, but all while still singing the Blues!

Family is what makes Everton the People's Club.
