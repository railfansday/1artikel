Goodbye, cavities? Scientists create gel that could regrow tooth enamel

📅 2026-11-05  |  Read Source →

Scientists have developed a new gel that could make it possible to regrow damaged tooth enamel, potentially overcoming one of the biggest limitations in dental care. Unlike bones and many other tissues in the body, tooth enamel cannot naturally regenerate once it has been lost. The new treatment offers a promising way to rebuild this protective layer, strengthen teeth, and help prevent future decay.

The innovation comes from researchers at the University of Nottingham's School of Pharmacy and Department of Chemical and Environmental Engineering. Their new material is inspired by the biological processes that form tooth enamel during early childhood. It is designed to repair enamel that has been weakened by mineral loss or worn away by erosion, while also reinforcing enamel that remains healthy.

The findings, published in Nature Communications, could pave the way for a new generation of dental treatments that go beyond protecting existing enamel and actually help restore its structure.

One of the most promising features of the technology is its simplicity. Dentists could apply the gel directly to teeth using a procedure similar to existing fluoride treatments, making it potentially easy to incorporate into routine dental care.

Unlike conventional fluoride products, however, the new gel contains no fluoride. Instead, it uses a material made from proteins that imitates important characteristics of the natural proteins responsible for guiding enamel formation when teeth first develop.

Once applied, the gel forms a thin but durable coating that penetrates the tooth's surface and fills microscopic cracks and gaps. This coating then provides a framework for rebuilding the mineral structure of enamel.

The process relies on calcium and phosphate ions, two essential mineral components naturally present in saliva. The gel attracts these minerals and helps organize them into new crystals that grow in an orderly pattern.

Scientists call this process epitaxial mineralization. Essentially, it means that new mineral crystals grow in alignment with the existing enamel, allowing the newly formed material to become structurally integrated with the original tooth surface.

This organization is especially important because enamel gets its exceptional hardness and durability from the precise arrangement of its mineral crystals. Simply depositing minerals on a tooth is not enough to recreate the complex structure that makes natural enamel so effective.

By guiding crystal growth, the new gel can restore both the microscopic architecture and the physical properties of healthy enamel.

A Potential Solution for Tooth Sensitivity and Decay

The researchers also discovered that their material can be used on exposed dentine, commonly called dentin in the United States. Dentin is the layer of tooth tissue beneath the enamel. It is softer than enamel and contains tiny channels that can transmit sensations toward the nerves inside the tooth.

When enamel wears away, or gums recede, exposed dentin can make teeth particularly sensitive to hot, cold, sweet, or acidic foods and drinks.

The new gel can encourage the formation of an enamel-like mineral layer over exposed dentin. This could help reduce tooth sensitivity while also improving the attachment of dental restorations, such as fillings and other treatments used to repair damaged teeth.

The potential benefits are significant. Enamel deterioration is a major factor in tooth decay and contributes to dental problems that affect nearly half of the world's population. These conditions can cause pain, infections, and eventually tooth loss. Poor oral health is also associated with broader health problems, including diabetes and cardiovascular disease.

One of the greatest challenges in dentistry is that enamel has no living cells capable of rebuilding it after teeth have fully developed. Although saliva can naturally replace some minerals lost during the earliest stages of enamel damage, the body cannot regenerate substantial amounts of enamel that have been worn away.

Existing treatments, including fluoride varnishes and remineralization products, can help protect teeth and repair early mineral loss. However, they cannot fully reconstruct enamel that has already been lost.

The new technology aims to overcome that limitation by encouraging the formation of new mineral tissue with properties resembling those of the original enamel.

Regenerated Enamel Performs Like Natural Tooth Enamel

The research team tested the mechanical strength and durability of the regenerated material under conditions designed to mimic everyday use.

These experiments examined how the newly formed enamel responded to activities and exposures that teeth routinely experience, including brushing, chewing, and contact with acidic foods.

The results were encouraging. According to the researchers, the regenerated enamel displayed mechanical properties comparable to those of healthy natural enamel, suggesting that the material could provide more than a temporary protective coating.

Dr. Abshar Hasan, a postdoctoral fellow and the study's lead author, explained why recreating enamel's internal structure is so important.

"Dental enamel has a unique structure, which gives enamel its remarkable properties that protect our teeth throughout life against physical, chemical, and thermal insults. When our material is applied to demineralized or eroded enamel, or exposed dentine, the material promotes the growth of crystals in an integrated and organized manner, recovering the architecture of our natural healthy enamel. We have tested the mechanical properties of these regenerated tissues under conditions simulating 'real-life situations' such as tooth brushing, chewing, and exposure to acidic foods, and found that the regenerated enamel behaves just like healthy enamel."

These findings suggest that the gel could eventually offer a way to restore the strength and protective function of damaged enamel rather than simply slowing further deterioration. However, the laboratory results do not yet establish how well the treatment will perform over extended periods in patients.

Beyond its ability to regenerate enamel, the researchers emphasize that the technology was developed with practical dental applications in mind.

The gel is designed to be safe, simple to apply, and suitable for manufacturing on a larger scale. Its versatility could also allow it to be incorporated into several types of dental products, potentially benefiting patients of different ages with enamel erosion, tooth sensitivity, or other problems involving exposed dentin.

Professor Alvaro Mata, Chair in Biomedical Engineering & Biomaterials at the University of Nottingham, led the study and highlighted the potential for translating the discovery into products that dentists can use.

"We are very excited because the technology has been designed with the clinician and patient in mind. It is safe, can be easily and rapidly applied, and it is scalable. Also, the technology is versatile, which opens the opportunity to be translated into multiple types of products to help patients of all ages suffering from a variety of dental problems associated with loss of enamel and exposed dentine. We have started this process with our start-up company Mintech-Bio and hope to have a first product out by next year; this innovation could soon be helping patients worldwide."

The researchers have already begun working toward commercialization through their company, Mintech-Bio, with the goal of introducing an initial product as early as the year following the announcement.

Although further development and clinical validation will be important before the technology becomes an established dental treatment, its ability to recreate the structure and strength of natural enamel represents a promising advance.

If the approach proves successful in patients, it could fundamentally change how dentists address enamel loss, opening the possibility of rebuilding tooth surfaces that previously could only be protected or restored using conventional dental materials.

The Zelda: Ocarina Of Time Easter Eggs That Did And Did Not Make It Into The 2026 Remake

📅 2026-11-05  |  Read Source →

The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time for the Nintendo 64 is a game full of Easter Eggs, secrets, and hidden mechanics (did you know if a butterfly lands on your Deku Stick, it summons a fairy?). Now in its second remake, not every secret has made its way forward through the previous iterations, but many have. Below you will learn about a few of the secrets that made the cut, and others that didn’t.

In the courtyard where Link first meets Zelda, there is arguably the most fourth-wall-breaking Easter Egg in the whole game. If you don’t rush to Zelda and instead pause and enter first-person to look around, you will see portraits of other Nintendo characters through the windows, like Mario and Yoshi. In the 3DS remake, the portraits are replaced by an image of a Mario stage.

For the 2026 remake, there are no references to other Nintendo characters. Instead, the tapestry you build over the course of the game is on display. Removing the reference to Nintendo characters outside of the Zelda universe is understandable, but it is a little odd that art representing the full adventure of the game – an adventure that has not yet concluded – is on the wall.

As stated above, the Nintendo character portraits are gone, but there is still a guard you can annoy. If you fire a Deku Seed into the left window, a guard will pop out, yell at you, and then throw a bomb at you. You can’t pick up the bomb. In fact, at this point in the game, you haven’t even seen a bomb.

The Hyrule Castle portrait references to Yoshi and Mario are gone (though Mario and Luigi are still kind of in the game in a way), but Bowser gets a pass for some reason. Both Malon and her dad, Talon, are still wearing Bowser pins on their clothes. And unlike the N64 game where the detail was a little vague, there is no mistaking it here.

Gossip Stones are all over Ocarina of Time and it takes a while to understand what they do. If you hit them with a sword, they tell you the time, and if you play Zelda’s Lullaby near them, they refill your magic. If you do the Happy Mask Salesman’s side quest, interacting with them while wearing the Mask of Truth offers little hints and details about the world. But then there are interactions that are just odd for no reason. Blowing up a bomb near a Gossip Stone still makes it take off like a rocket ship, and using the Megaton Hammer still flattens them for a moment like a Looney Tunes.

And speaking of playing Zelda’s Lullaby for inanimate objects, if you slash a sign to pieces with your sword and regret your words and deeds, you can play the iconic tune to make the signs reform and become readable again.

This detail was exclusive to the 3DS remake of Ocarina of Time, and surprising no one, it has been removed. Ocarina of Time launched in June 2011 and Nintendo released Skyward Sword about five months later in November. For… marketing purposes, I guess, Skyward Sword art could be found in Lonlon Ranch, the Bombchu Shop in Hyrule Castle Town, and the Gerudo Fortress. It was a fun Easter Egg to discover back in 2011, especially since Skyward Sword was still months away, but these days it’s a bit of an odd inclusion.

Though never explicit, the composer ghosts guarding the Sun’s Song (and Link’s first ReDead encounter) have always been assumed to be inspired by Mario and Luigi. They are brothers; Flat is short, squat, and red; Sharp is lanky and green. They still appear in Ocarina of Time this way, which isn't surprising, since they are important characters you must meet to get a very useful song. Fans have also speculated that Talon and Ingo are Mario and Luigi-inspired for similar reasons, but it’s worth noting that they are not brothers.

In the Spirit Temple there is a large statue of a woman and for reasons that have never been explained, Link could attach his hookshot to her breasts. It served no purpose. It didn’t help solve a puzzle. If anything, it was confusing. But we all tried it and there is no shame in admitting it! That was removed for the 3DS remake, and it remains removed in 2026.

This is a very subtle reference to Wind Waker/Breath of the Wild/Tears of the Kingdom and is potentially the only addition to the remake that looks toward the future of the Zelda franchise. Obviously, plenty of characters in Ocarina later appear in Majora’s Mask (another remake of that is hopefully being considered), but it would be inaccurate to call those intentional Easter Eggs.

However, in Kokiri Forest and in the neighboring Lost Woods, the magical debris floating in the air often resembles a Korok as it would later appear in the series. It makes sense, too, because the Koroks are meant to be what the Kokiri transformed into following The Great Flood.

Alpine Linux Security Advisory: OpenSSH-server password authentication bypass

📅 2026-11-05  |  Read Source →

If you run sshd on Alpine Linux, update openssh now and restart sshd. Upgrading the package alone does not replace the running daemon.

If you cannot update yet, at least disable password logins: set PasswordAuthentication no in your sshd configuration and restart sshd. Either one closes the hole.

An attacker who can reach your SSH port can log in without knowing any password. It takes one connection, works every time, and needs no retries.

The attacker needs the name of an account whose password field is * (an account with no password set, which is normal for key-only accounts), and needs the machine to have at least one other account with a $5$ or $6$ password hash. The attacker then sends a very long password (257 to 1024 bytes). One user with a password is enough: it makes every account without one reachable.

Details are in the OpenSSH 10.6p2 release notes. No CVE identifier had been assigned at the time of writing.

The official tiny-cloud images (the qcow2, VHD and AMI artifacts) from 3.18.11 through 3.24.3 ship an alpine user with a * password field and passwordless doas. They are safe on first boot. They become exploitable as soon as any $6$ hash exists on the machine, for example when an admin sets a console password, or user-data adds a user with hashed_passwd. An attacker then gets a shell as alpine and can use doas to become root.

Images built from 3.21.10, 3.22.8, 3.23.8, 3.24.4 and later include the fixed package. Images from earlier point releases do not, so on every running instance, update openssh or disable password authentication.

These releases have the same bug. 3.21 reached its end of support on 2026-11-01, but we have published fixed packages for each release all the way back to 3.16.

Look in /var/log/messages for sshd lines reading Accepted password for an account that has no password (alpine, root, or any key-only user). Review doas use and recently created users, files and authorized keys on exposed machines. If you cannot rule out a login, treat the machine as compromised.

When sshd is built without PAM, it checks a password by hashing the attempt with crypt() (the C library function that turns a password into its stored hash) and comparing the result to the stored password field. For an account whose stored field is too short to contain a salt (the random prefix of a hash), sshd borrows the salt of another account.

musl’s crypt() returns the string * as an error indicator when the key is over 256 bytes long. * is also exactly what the stored field contains for every account with no password, so the “hash” matches and the login succeeds.

OpenSSH 10.6p2 avoids the problem in sshd. The stable-branch packages above carry a patch for the same problem.

Here's a rundown of changes to some well-known tax provisions for the 2027 tax year.

Each year, the IRS updates common tax provisions, such as income tax brackets and the standard deduction, to ensure the tax code is keeping pace with the rising cost of living.

These tweaks — formally known as inflation adjustments — help prevent “bracket creep,” where taxpayers who received a cost-of-living raise can pay more in taxes even though their purchasing power remains unchanged.

Inflation adjustments for the 2027 tax year were set at 3.2% — larger than the previous two years as inflation heated up. This could mean lower tax bills for some people when they file in 2028.

In the U.S., there are seven marginal rates — 10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35% and 37% — which will remain unchanged for 2027.

Contrary to a popular myth, it’s uncommon for people to pay a single flat federal tax rate on their taxable income. Instead, portions of a person’s income can be taxed at different rates depending on which tax brackets they fall into. For example, some of a person’s income could be taxed at 10%, another chunk at 12%, and so on.

For 2027, the IRS is adjusting those tax brackets — the windows of income that dictate where a tax rate ends and begins — for each filing status. Biggest MLB offseason questions: How long will lockout last? Will we see a free agency frenzy?

Not so long ago, the Milwaukee Brewers were crowned World Series champions, and at the moment of the last out, the 2026-27 Major League offseason began. So let's talk about that.

The winter ahead is one shot through with uncertainty, as labor strife figures to roil the sport and perhaps even compromise the 2027 season. Those matters, vital as they are, won't be the only story, and we'll still have the familiar presences of free agency and trade talk to sustain us.

To set the scene for all that's ahead in advance of the 2027 campaign – good, ill, or somewhere in the middle – we're here to take a look at the five questions that will inform an offseason that's going to be quite unusual and – for good, ill, or somewhere in the middle – probably unforgettable. Let's explore those five queries right now …

The current collective bargaining agreement – or CBA, the accord that governs the working relationship between clubs/owners and players – is set to expire at 11:59 p.m. ET on Dec. 1 of this year. At that time, MLB owners are all but certain to lock out the players. That's a labor stoppage, and that means the offseason will be brought to a sudden halt. The lockout, the purpose of which is to preempt a players' strike, will last until a new CBA is agreed to. Agreeing to a new CBA will be no simple matter, and owners and their hireling, commissioner Rob Manfred, are pushing for a cap on team payrolls. The players, as represented by their union, the MLBPA, have never shown any willingness to agree to a cap-and-floor system. There are a lot of other issues to iron out, as the CBA is an all-encompassing document, but the fight over a cap will be what drags out the lockout.

The heart of the matter is whether the lockout will last long enough to push back, abbreviate, or – perish the thought – cancel entirely the 2027 season. The latter is the worst-case scenario and is highly unlikely, but it's a possibility, however remote. More of a threat is a 2027 season that doesn't span the usual 162 games.

We have a recent precedent to guide us. The last time the CBA was up for negotiation during the offseason of 2021-22, owners locked out the players for 99 days. That meant a shortened spring training by about two and a half weeks and forced the regular season calendar to be compressed, albeit while still preserving the 162-game docket. As such, we can say that if the lockout lasts beyond the middle of March 2027, then we'll likely lose regular-season games.

Just two, the 1981 and 1994-95 player strikes, significantly compromised seasons. The 1994-95 stoppage cost us the 1994 World Series, and it took years for the sport to recover from the resulting fan disaffection.

As for the 2027 season, the worst-case scenario may not be the loss of the entire season but rather the loss of 60 games off the regular-season slate. Here's the explanation from our recent Lockout 101 piece:

"… [I]f the lockout extends into late May or so and costs at least 60 games, then most of the league's national broadcast and streaming contracts would be extended for another year, through 2029. That matters because Manfred and the league hope to bundle and centralize team streaming rights and share those revenues equally, and the current timeline would theoretically allow that to happen at the same time the national contracts are up for renegotiation in 2028. Tacking on another year to those national contracts would push back the significant windfall that owners and the league are expecting from that new media rights structure. This amounts to a major incentive, at least on the league side, to agree to a new CBA before June arrives."

Obviously, the hope is that the lockout doesn't even affect the spring training calendar, let alone cost us a major chunk of the regular season.

As noted, the big fight is over whether or not MLB will have a cap-and-floor structure moving forward, as is the case in the NFL, NBA, and NHL. Owners have tied every single proposal wrinkle to date to the players' acceptance of a cap. However, it remains highly unlikely, at least until further notice, that the union would ever agree to such a thing.

The other major issue is a likely restructuring of the revenue-sharing system. Specifically, the next CBA is highly likely to revise the system so that local broadcast revenues are shared much more evenly. Fortunately, there's already some common ground on this issue, and a reordering of that system would directly address the revenue disparities within MLB that are such a reliable source of consternation – and misinformation – without the grievous labor war that a continued cap push would force upon us.

There's a lot to sort out, to say the least. The expectation is that the next CBA is going to lead to sweeping changes to the sport starting in 2027 and, perhaps by design, growing over time. Even if there's no cap and floor, the economic structure of MLB is almost certain to look very different after this winter.

Overall, this is one of the weakest free agent classes in living memory, but there is elite superstar value to be found at the tip-top with back-to-back (for the moment) American League Cy Young winner Tarik Skubal.

Skubal, who turns 30 on Nov. 20, remains foremost in the discussion for best pitcher in baseball, and he's going to be paid like it. The expectation is that he'll wind up signing the largest pitcher contract in MLB history and perhaps also fetch the highest-ever annual salary for a pitcher. It's not out of the question that Skubal's next contract could approach or even surpass $400 million in total value. At those rates, the incumbent Los Angeles Dodgers and the free-spending New York Mets are the consensus favorites. The "when" of all this is more complicated thanks to the upcoming lockout detailed above. Will premium free agents and their representatives rush to find some stability in advance of the stoppage, during which no major-league transactions will be permitted, or will they decide that the long game will be better for their markets? It's possible that the biggest spenders on the team side will look to make moves early, as the barrel of the next CBA figures to be leveled directly at them and their abundant revenues. Or maybe the unknown will have a chilling effect on spending before we arrive at the lockout.

The past is not always prelude, but let's note that we saw a flurry of spending leading up to the lockout of 2021-22. Indeed, in November 2021, MLB teams committed more than $1.7 billion to free agents prior to the lockout. The Texas Rangers alone guaranteed $500 million to a new middle infield – Corey Seager and Marcus Semien – before the lockout put an end to such things. In all, teams put ink to paper on five nine-figure contracts prior to the 2021-22 lockout.

The comparisons may not entirely hold since this upcoming CBA should bring about a much more drastic re-ordering of the sport than the current expiring CBA did. As such, teams may proceed cautiously given all the uncertainties. Or, as just mentioned, they may seek to get business done before the ways of doing business change entirely. We'll see soon enough. If we do see the dollars start flying before the lockout, then the upcoming Nov. 10-12 general managers meetings in Las Vegas may occasion the groundwork for all those deals.

Will a lackluster free agent class mean more headline-grabbing trades? That may indeed be answered in the affirmative this winter. Yes, the CBA uncertainty will have a bearing on trades, especially those that involve the assumption of major salary obligations, but big trades still figure to be a prominent part of this offseason.

It's of course impossible to know who's going to be dealt ahead of time, but some big names perhaps on the move this winter include Francisco Lindor, Mason Miller, Spencer Strider, Hunter Greene, Otto Lopez, Jeremy Peña, Ketel Marte, Jacob deGrom, and of course others. Paul Skenes? Almost certainly not, but you'll just as certainly hear his name bandied about.

Turning back to last lockout, it was mostly minor trades before the sport shut down. Yes, we'll see big ones this time around, but they may not happen until we have a new CBA in place and the lockout is over.

John Calipari has coached plenty of outstanding one-and-done guards during his time at Memphis, Kentucky and Arkansas, including Gilgeous-Alexander, Devin Booker, Derrick Rose, De'Aaron Fox, Jamal Murray, John Wall, and Tyrese Maxey. Simply put: if you're an elite high school guard prospect, you want to play for Coach Cal.

Acuff's season at Arkansas had him in the conversation for the best freshman guard Calipari has coached. Does that mean he's going to become a two-time NBA MVP like SGA or a multi-time NBA All-Star like the other names on that list? The jury is still out, but his college production — on both ends of the floor — is similar to what we are seeing play out in the NBA.

For starters, Arkansas didn't have a deep roster, which meant Acuff was on the floor a lot. He played just over 35 minutes per game, which is slightly less than he's playing with Sacramento right now. The same depth issues Acuff experienced at Arkansas are what he's dealing with in the NBA with his new team.

The Kings don't have a true backup point guard, which means Acuff is playing more than anybody else on the roster. When Acuff comes off the floor, Sacramento has been playing Malik Monk as the de facto point guard — a role he has filled in spot minutes throughout his time with the Kings.

Ben Simmons was also expected to be in Sacramento's starting lineup this season, where he could've taken on some of the ball-handling duties in the first and second units, but he suffered a torn Achilles days before the 2026-27 season began. That leaves Acuff, most nights, to play the entire fourth quarter and run the offense with a team full of players who can't create their own shot.

While Acuff's offensive game is as good as anybody in his class, the knock on him during the pre-draft process was his defense. It showed up throughout his time at Arkansas, but in Acuff's defense (no pun intended), he was asked to be the primary engine of the offense while staying on the floor for most of the game.

In Sacramento, it's been more of the same.

Acuff isn't a strong defender, and projecting him as a net-neutral defender over the course of his career would be a big win for his development. The offensive end is where Acuff is going to make his money. His processing skills for his age are elite and will only get better.

Acuff to Sacramento was one of the worst-kept secrets throughout the entire pre-draft cycle. The connections between his family and Kings general manager Scott Perry run deep: Perry coached Darius Acuff Sr. when he played at Eastern Kentucky in the 1990s.

In what proceeded to be a rather tame opening half, Marseille were first to show signs of life. Amine Gouiri slipped the ball through Jarell Quansah's legs, but failed to get the better of Mark Flekken. Otherwise, chances were limited for both sides who failed to land punches.

Leverkusen found trouble in the final third, looking incapable of getting into threatening positions. The Rhinelanders required all of 28 minutes before Patrik Schick set up Quansah. However, the centre-half could only muster up a defender's finish which cleared the crossbar.

Ibrahim Maza soon found room for a central shot that failed to trouble Jeffrey de Lange. Had it gone in, the offside flag was already raised against Schick. The hosts were denied by the width of the bar before the break as Miguel Gutierrez's hit took a nasty deflection.

The second-half took on a similar life to the first until Igor Paixão and Gouiri unfurled shots that kept Flekken on his toes. Leverkusen's dominance prior to the interval appeared to have been relinquished as Jeoffrey Kondogbia was inches away from breaking the deadlock.

A goal eventually arrived, falling the way of the Ligue 1 side with 13 minutes remaining. Aleix Garcia will wince watching it back, letting the ball get away from him. Gouiri proceeded to race free and cut in before bending a left-footed shot beyond Flekken's despairing fingertips.

Leverkusen's sense of urgency ramped up as Edmond Tapsoba misfired from a corner. De Lange flapped at it, but made amends at the death by holding onto Schick's stoppage time header. A result which sealed a precious first victory in Marseille's Europa League odyssey.
