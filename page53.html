An open letter to Newcastle United fans

📅 2026-11-09  |  Read Source →

An open letter to my fellow Newcastle United fans – The 12th man.

‘We call ourselves the best supporters in the world. We wear our black and white stripes with pride. We sing about loyalty, passion, history and what it means to be a Geordie. We talk about St James’ Park being a fortress, about the noise, the atmosphere and the incredible connection between this club and its supporters.

Are we actually living up to the names we give ourselves?

Because, if I’m being honest, what we’ve witnessed at St James’ Park, particularly towards the back end of last season and continuing into this new chapter, has been difficult to watch. Not because of the football alone, but because of the attitude coming from the stands.

The atmosphere has, at times, become toxic. The groans, the negativity, the frustration, the booing at half-time and full-time. Players being written off before they’ve had a proper opportunity to settle. A new team being judged against the standards of a team that took years to build.

And I can’t help thinking that as Newcastle United fans we’ve lost sight of something incredibly important.

Look at what has happened at this football club. We’ve experienced significant change. A new manager, the loss of our club captain, the departure of experienced senior players and leaders, both on and off the pitch. The familiar faces, personalities and voices that helped shape the identity of this team have changed.

The dressing room has changed. The leadership has changed. The dynamics have changed. The team is evolving.

And yet, almost before this new group has had the opportunity to find its feet, we’ve been quick to turn on them.

Did we think a new manager would walk through the door, inherit a changing squad and everything would immediately click into place? That new partnerships, understanding, confidence and leadership would magically appear overnight?

Players need time to understand each other. New leaders need time to emerge. A manager needs time to implement his ideas. Confidence needs nurturing, not destroying.

And that last point is particularly important.

Imagine being a player walking out at St James’ Park, knowing that one misplaced pass, one poor decision or one disappointing half could be met with a chorus of groans or boos.

Imagine trying to build confidence in an unfamiliar team when the very people who are supposed to lift you are making you feel ten feet tall one minute and ten feet under the next.

We all want to win. We all want to see Newcastle competing at the highest level. Nobody is suggesting we should accept poor performances, abandon ambition or pretend everything is perfect when it isn’t.

We’re football supporters. We have every right to be frustrated, disappointed and demanding.

But there is a difference between demanding better and actively contributing to making things worse.

There is a difference between supporting a football club and only supporting it when things are going well.

This is Newcastle United. We know our history. We’ve experienced the highs, the lows, the heartbreak and the near misses. We’ve stood on the terraces through some truly difficult times. We know what loyalty means.

So why, now that the club is trying to build something, are we so quick to turn on the very people representing it?

The irony is that we constantly talk about the incredible atmosphere at St James’ Park. Opposition supporters talk about it. Players talk about it. Managers talk about it. It’s one of the things that makes Newcastle United special.

But that atmosphere isn’t created by the architecture, the pitch or the players alone.

We are the 12th man. Not a marketing slogan. Not something we sing about when we’re winning. Not something we switch on for the big European nights and switch off when a game isn’t going our way.

When the team is struggling, that’s when they need us most. When confidence is low, that’s when the noise should be loudest. When a new player makes a mistake, that’s when we should be getting behind him, not making him dread his next touch.

Imagine what it could do for a young player, a new signing or someone finding their feet in a different role to hear 52,000 people behind them rather than against them.

Imagine the confidence that could give a team still learning to play together.

We pride ourselves on being different. On being passionate, fiercely loyal and united behind our club. Well, perhaps it’s time we started showing it again.

Let’s stop comparing every new player with someone who has left. Let’s stop expecting a team in transition to perform like a settled side. Let’s give the manager and his players the opportunity to build relationships, develop an identity and find their rhythm.

And, above all, let’s remember that supporting Newcastle United isn’t conditional on the scoreline.

There will be poor performances. There will be mistakes. There will be frustrating afternoons, and Monday night catastrophes. There will be games when we leave St James’ Park wondering what on earth we’ve just watched.

But we should never allow frustration to become the defining feature of our support.

We have an opportunity here. A new chapter, a new group of players and a chance to build something together. The club has changed, and perhaps it’s time we changed our approach too.

The players have a responsibility to give everything for that shirt. The manager has a responsibility to get the best out of his squad. The board has a responsibility to take the club forward.

And we, the supporters, have a responsibility too.

Our job is to make St James’ Park a place opposition teams fear and our own players feel inspired.

So the next time we go through the turnstiles, let’s think about what we’re bringing into that stadium.

Let’s sing a little louder. Let’s get behind the players. Let’s lift them when they’re struggling. Let’s make some noise when things aren’t going our way.

Let’s be the supporters we constantly tell the rest of the world we are.

Because being the best fans in the world isn’t something you earn when your team wins.

It’s something you prove when they need you most.

We are Newcastle United. We are the Toon Army. We are the 12th man.

It’s about time we started acting like it.’

Wouter Vrancken condemned a “ridiculous” first-half display from his sorry Hearts side as their 18-month unbeaten home record in the William Hill Premiership was ripped apart by ruthless Falkirk.

The beleaguered Jambos, who went into the game without 11 players through injury following a gruelling run of fixtures, endured a dismal afternoon as the Bairns raced into a 4-0 half-time lead on their way to a 5-2 victory at Tynecastle.

Finley Barbrook, Owen Eames, Liam Henderson and Jacob Murrell’s double had the visitors 5-0 up within 47 minutes before Hearts reduced the deficit through substitute Sabri Guendouz and a Claudio Braga penalty.

It was a first league defeat at Tynecastle since April 2025 for the Jambos, who have now won just two of their last 11 matches in all competitions.

“This is an embarrassment, the first half for sure,” said Vrancken. “Always doubting, always doing things half-half, in possession, out of possession, everything, and such easy goals again.

“When you give those easy goals, then you cannot win a game. It’s childish goals and cannot happen on this level. Those simple things, they are the basics actually in these situations.

“It’s crazy, crazy, and everybody – me, staff, players – have to take responsibility in those things. This is ridiculous.”

To exacerbate their grim predicament, the Jambos lost yet another senior player as defender Malachi Fagan-Walcott limped off in the second half.

“This also makes me a bit angry, because now we lose Mal again,” said Vrancken.

“Hopefully it’s not too bad, but still we had to replace him there, because he was one of the guys who were good in the game and in the last games actually he had a really consistent, good level.”

Hearts were leapfrogged into third place by Falkirk, who made it five top-flight wins in a row for the first time in 31 years.

“I’ve never been 5-0 up here before,” said Bairns manager John McGlynn. “First half we were outstanding. A great start to the game for us to get the goal so early.

“It was well documented about the Hearts injuries and, coming off the back of the game on Thursday night (in Thun), it was always going to be difficult for them.

Toto Wolff says he's "proud" of Mercedes' turnaround to win the 2026 Formula 1 Constructors' Championship and feels very few sports teams have bounced back so quickly.

Mercedes won their ninth constructors' title on Sunday at the Sao Paulo Grand Prix, five years after their last championship, which was their eighth in a row at the time.

Regulation changes in 2022 ended Mercedes' dominance as they struggled to get on top of the so-called ground effect cars, but they have won the first season of the new 2026 cars, and also wrapped up the Drivers' Championship earlier this month with Kimi Antonelli.

"The thing is when you are in these difficult moments, like we all are sometimes in our lives, it's not that easy to keep going," said Wolff.

"Looking back in retrospect, you're saying, 'we needed these tough moments in order to grow as people or to grow as an organisation'. But when you're down, that is quite difficult to feel.

"I think those last four years were very difficult between getting it wrong, then kind of understanding why it was wrong and then realising no we didn't understand. That was a bit back and forth.

"But we will be looking back in 10 or 20 years. When you look at the statistics, it wasn't that we were complete losers over those four years, because we finished second, (second), third, fourth [in the championship], we won races, won many races. So it wasn't that bad at all when you look at the sheer numbers, but it felt not good."

"It feels really good sealing those two titles. Not many sports teams have done that after consecutive run of championships, then a difficult spell and then coming back, so I'm really proud that we are part of this very small group of teams that have done that in all sports."

Australia drop Labuschagne for New Zealand Test series

📅 2026-11-09  |  Read Source →

Australia axe Marnus Labuschagne with batter averaging less than 24 in 10 Tests since returning to the side for last winter's Ashes; chair of selectors George Bailey does not rule out 32-year-old being recalled for tour to India early next year and next summer's Ashes series in England

Australia have dropped Marnus Labuschagne for the four-Test series at home to New Zealand.

The squad has not yet been announced for a contest that begins in Perth on December 9, but chair of selectors George Bailey has revealed that batter Labuschagne will not be selected after averaging only 23.66 in 10 Tests since returning for last winter's Ashes.

The 32-year-old mustered just 131 runs across three Tests at an average of 21.83 during Australia's recent 2-1 series defeat in South Africa and has gone 49 innings without a Test century, stretching back to the 2023 Ashes series in England.

Bailey told reporters: "We won't be considering Marn for selection.

"I just wanted to clear that up as there's going to be, or has been, a lot of speculation around his place in the team. I think this gives him a little bit of space and clears up a bit of conjecture."

Bailey did not rule out Labuschagne forcing his way back in for the tour of India early next year or next summer's Ashes series.

He said: "It's still possible, absolutely. I think we all know how good and how effective Marnus can be at his best. But that performance hasn't been there.

"He's pragmatic. He knows that the fundamental nature of being a batter in the top six is to score runs and that just hasn't happened."

Labuschagne, who struck 10 centuries in his first 30 Tests and was once ranked No 1 in the ICC batting rankings, was briefly dropped for last year's tour of the West Indies, only to force his way back in for the 2025-26 Ashes after five hundreds in 10 innings for state side Queensland across red-ball and one-day cricket.

Bailey added of the right-hander, who has notched only one hundred in his last 38 Tests: "He's always been an incredible performer at first-class level, so I expect that that'll be the case [again].

"There's no message to go back and do anything in particular. I think the currency is pretty obvious. The plan is just to go back and focus on playing as well as he can for Queensland for a period of time."

After playing New Zealand in Perth, Australia will face the Black Caps in Adelaide, Melbourne and Sydney before taking on India away in five Tests from January 21.

The team's next assignment is a series of white-ball games at home to England, with the three-match ODI leg starting in Perth on Friday ahead of five T20s from November 21.
