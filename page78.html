West Ham United are set to secure the best possible fit for their managerial role in Scott Parker.

The East London outfit have reportedly already reached an agreement for Parker to take charge of the team, after they opted to sack Nuno Espirito Santo on Tuesday morning.

Wyness believes that Parker is the perfect man to guide West Ham through the "hand-to-hand combat" of the Championship.

The former Fulham and Burnley boss has a 100 per cent record when it comes to achieving promotion from the Championship, having led Fulham, Bournemouth and Burnley into the top flight on their first attempts.

West Ham will now be hoping to repeat that trick, and Wyness is optimistic about the former midfielder's chances of turning the ship around.

Everton’s former chief Wyness – who served as CEO at Goodison Park between 2004 and 2009 and now runs a football consultancy advising elite clubs – believes that West Ham had a move for Parker lined up and ready to go before they settled on their decision to sack Nuno.

The boardroom expert believes that the incoming boss has the right "attitude" to spark the relentless form that is needed from a promotion hopeful.

Speaking on the latest edition of Football Insider's Inside Track podcast, Wyness made it clear that he has no doubt that Parker is the right fit for West Ham.

"West Ham are in a sprint, and I think that Parker is probably the right one. As you say, he knows the club. He's had over 100 appearances for West Ham as well," he said.

"He's exactly the type of street fighter that you need in the Championship, and he'll light a fire under the players we were just talking about. I think his style and his attitude are probably the right ones to come in right now.

"There's no time to mess around building and making plans. I'm afraid it's hand-to-hand combat in the Championship, and Scott's up for it, and I think it's as good a decision as they can make.

"I heard a lot of other names mentioned, but they seem to have done all their checks, and it was ready to go before they fired Nuno, so it does seem Scott's going to get it."

The ex-recruitment man feels that the Argentine's displays have been "nowhere near" top-flight level of late.

Brown believes that West Ham need an "out-and-out striker" to lead the line, and Castellanos is not doing enough for them right now.

He expects them to address their concerns in January, with a replacement frontman likely to come through the door.

Manchester United came back from 2-0 down to beat AS Roma 3-2 in the Champions League on Tuesday night.

The Red Devils looked set for yet another defeat as Michael Carrick faced the pressure.

A second-half turnaround would ultimately take plenty of heat off the Englishman, although his future still remains in question at least for now.

The comeback could prove to be a significant turning point for Carrick, particularly after United's recent struggles.

But one result will not remove any concerns that have been had over the manager's abilities.

Especially as referee Jesus Gil Manzano may have had quite a role to play by not quite covering himself in glory at Old Trafford.

The start of Manchester United's comeback emerged from Matheus Cunha going down in the box, and a penalty being awarded.

The Brazilian looked to race through on goal, with defender Gianluca Mancini deemed to have fouled him.

However, on replays, while there was a hand on his back, the contact was not enough for the forward to have hit the floor.

And as you continue to watch the incident, you do then notice that Cunha had actually seemingly tripped himself up.

The hand on the back may have meant the incident did not meet the threshold for VAR intervention, but it should never have been given as a penalty in the first place.

The next blunder would not come too long after the decision to award Manchester United a penalty.

As Bruno Fernandes stepped up to take the spot-kick, the Portuguese midfielder would bluff his lines, with goalkeeper Devis Vazquez saving the attempt.

Manchester United would then find a stroke of luck, with the penalty ordered to be retaken due to encroachment.

However, encroachment rules state there must be a material impact on the penalty for it to come into effect.

In this incident, those who had entered the box early were on the opposite side of the box to where the penalty was saved.

And were nowhere to be seen when the ball was cleared following the save.

They had no impact on the outcome, meaning the first penalty attempt should have stood.

Man United probably should have had another penalty late on as well, with Shea Lacey fouled inside the box.

As the United academy product attempted to cross the ball, a late challenge wiped him out inside the area by Roma defender Wesley.

The incident was completely waved off, with the score at the time being 2-2.

United would eventually end up getting that much-needed third goal anyway.

The 24-year-old's first significant mistake came when he dropped Alex Iwobi's corner into his own net on Saturday, throwing away the lead as Villa drew 1-1 with Fulham.

Sebastien Pocognoli has told his Scotland players that their grace period is over following his maiden camp, and that they have to produce better displays and results when they take on North Macedonia and Switzerland to round off their Nations League group campaign.

Pocognoli was willing to give his men a little leeway in the condensed four-game sequence that kicked off his reign as manager, given that the squad included many new faces and the players were getting used to the preferred system and demands of their new head coach.

Scotland produced a creditable display, particularly in the second half, as they were unfortunate to be held to a goalless draw during Pocognoli’s first game in charge away to Slovenia, but they followed that up with a 3-0 loss to Switzerland at Hampden after Jack Hendry’s early red card.

They bounced back to beat North Macedonia 2-0 in Skopje, but rounded off the opening round of group fixtures with a disappointing 2-1 defeat to the Slovenians at Hampden.

That has left the Scots needing to win both of their remaining matches to have a realistic chance of qualifying for the playoffs for League A in March, and Pocognoli - speaking as he announced his latest squad - has left his players in no doubt over what he expects from them.

“The first camp was to get to know each other. I saw things in training and in the games, we produced some very good moments. We created a lot of chances, maybe more than in previous years against those types of opponents.

“We have a different system, different players and different approach to the game. I’m sure we can shine in that way but we need to make the standard higher and win games. That’s the idea for the next camp.

“We need to improve but every country who started with a new coach in September would say the same. Many players in the first camp took it as a new experience with a new style of play and system.

“That needs a little bit of time, but it doesn’t mean we can’t now ask for more and reach the level we want.

“We now have Che Adams back, Scott (McTominay) back, Kieran Tierney and we’re still missing Kenny McLean.

“These players bring stability. I’m pleased with this selection because we feel we have a good mix.”

No case for defence if Neil McCann can't turn around kamikaze Kilmarnock quickly

📅 2026-11-04  |  Read Source →

When Gary Neville’s Valencia side conceded a seventh goal to Barcelona, the camera cut to the Manchester United legend standing in his technical area looking ashen faced, utterly bereft.

As you took in his befuddled expression, helpless to prevent the car crash unfolding before him, you couldn’t help but wonder if he would have the gall to soon be back on the Sky Sports couch, telling others how to do the job he had just so miserably failed in.

Of course he did. And that spell in charge of what, admittedly, at the time, was an absolute basket case of a club, has been used as a stick with which to beat him ever since.

If things ultimately don’t work out for Neil McCann at Rugby Park – and it is starting to look as though that may be the case – I am sure he too will be back in the BBC Sportsound studio before you know it. And rightly so. McCann is an engaging and insightful pundit. The qualities that make a good, entertaining football analyst don’t necessarily make a top-class manager, though.

But then, I would say that. I too am paid to give my opinions on the beautiful game, and the highlight of my own managerial career – about half a season as player/manager of Motherwell Phoenix in the Carluke & District Sunday league – was a solitary 3-2 win amid a barrage of hungover batterings. Take that, Lanark Youth.

It would be massively unfair too and disrespectful in fact to dismiss McCann as a pundit turned manager, given his vast experience in the game as a hugely talented player and his previous managerial stints at Dundee, Inverness on an interim basis, as well as his time at Rangers as part of Barry Ferguson’s backroom team.

However, as McCann himself will know having been part of the Scottish football media set, his performance as manager of Kilmarnock will justifiably be subject to the same sort of scrutiny he used to apply to others, and at the risk of drawing a similarly icy stare to the one he fixed on former BBC colleague Chick Young at the weekend when he asked if McCann was feeling the pressure, any objective analysis would have to conclude that things are not going so peachily at present.

Killie currently sit in 11th place in the table, a point ahead of Hibs, who have a game in hand. Most concerningly, they are shipping goals for fun, conceding 2.3 per game so far this term, comfortably the worst record in the division. They have lost eight goals more than anyone else, letting in 28 across their 12 games to Motherwell and St Mirren’s 20. Their goal difference of -15 puts them seven worse off than the closest side, the Saints again, in that column.

McCann has been round the block often enough to know that the team who ships the most goals over the course of a campaign usually goes down. On only four occasions in the Premiership era have the side who conceded the most goals survived, with two of those doing so via the playoffs after finishing 11th. Never has the team with the worst defence in the league finished higher than 10th, which has also happened just twice.

Surely, stumbling to safety, whether that be through the playoffs or by scraping into tenth place, isn’t the limit of Kilmarnock’s ambitions this term? If they continue on their current trajectory, though, and carry on defending so poorly, then not only will that be their ceiling, it also seems a bit of a reach. Across McCann’s time at the club, in fact, Killie have conceded 53 goals in 22 league games, a record that is surely unsustainable.

It would be remiss not to mention though that many of those games came after he walked into an absolutely dire predicament in January following a run of 10 games without a victory under predecessor Stuart Kettlewell. That extended to 14 games under interim boss Kris Doolan before club owner Billy Bowie sent out the SOS to McCann and assistant Billy Dodds in early January.

Kilmarnock were also 11th at that time, just three points ahead of bottom side Livingston, who had a game in hand. Had you offered any Killie fan the playoff spot at that stage, they might have bitten your hand off like a, well, hungry squirrel, but McCann managed to turn things around despite the make-up of the squad not being suited to his preferred 4-2-3-1 formation, and he secured the Rugby Park side’s safety with a game to spare.

It is fair to say then that the many sceptics who gave his appointment in the winter window a frosty reception were mainly won over by the fine job he did in the remainder of the campaign.

There was a great deal of optimism around in the summer, in fact, that having had the opportunity to build a squad in his image, that the issues such as the leaky defence that had plagued Killie would be ironed out, and all the while, he would retain the front-footed approach that had been so refreshing during his first months in charge.

The balance, though, is still way off kilter. While Killie still look decent going forward at times and play some nice stuff, there is no way of dressing up their persistent issues at the back. Their defending at times is absolutely criminal, and you can't be in a position where you need to score three times in a game just to have a chance of winning.

McCann held his hands up to a naïve approach when Celtic came and plundered four first half goals in an eventual 5-1 win at Rugby Park back in August, but naïve barely covers the way that his team twice gave up a lead against 10-man St Johnstone on Saturday.

The second goal in particular had to be seen to be believed. A Killie corner somehow led, within seconds, to an opposition player running through on goal, with the set-up behind the ball leaving the hosts wide open. Even then, in fairness to the Killie manager, his defenders should have done much better when they got back at Joel Cotterill, but three retreating Killie men allowed him to waltz through on Nico Mantl unhindered before finding the net to howls of derision from the home stands.

And therein lies the growing issue for McCann. The boos are getting louder week upon week. While it could be argued that he deserves a bit of time to correct the defensive issues his team are facing after the escape act he pulled off last season, the more that his side display the same consistent weaknesses, the less faith there is from the Killie support that he can pull their team out of another mess. This time, one of his own making.

In the end, it won’t be his erstwhile colleagues in the media that seal his fate, but those paying – and increasingly, braying – punters. If he doesn’t want to be back at Pacific Quay any time soon, he has to find a way to bolt the back door.

There are no guarantees of course that changing manager mid-season would help pull Kilmarnock out of this death spiral they are currently in. Though, admittedly, it did work for them the last time they tried it.

Time is surely running out for McCann to avoid being on the other side of that scenario.
