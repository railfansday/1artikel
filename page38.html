📅 2026-11-05  |  Read Source →

Southwest Airlines has been named the least punctual of North America's 10 leading airlines in October 2026, with just 68.9% of its flights arriving on time. According to the latest Cirium On-Time Performance Report, almost one in three completed Southwest flights arrived at least 15 minutes behind schedule, putting the Dallas-based carrier well behind its competitors.

At the opposite end of the rankings, Delta Air Lines returned to first place with an on-time performance rate of 86.9%, continuing a remarkable recovery from a difficult summer. Southwest's performance comes amid sweeping changes to its business model, including checked-baggage fees, assigned seating, and greater aircraft utilization, raising questions about the operational consequences of its transformation.

Delta's October performance represented a significant turnaround from July, when only 75% of its flights arrived on time. That figure improved to 79.2% in August, 82.5% in September, and 86.7% in October, an increase of nearly 12 percentage points. The result also placed Delta third in Cirium's global rankings, behind Hainan Airlines and Aeromexico.

United Airlines ranked second in North America at 84.06%, followed by WestJet at 83.56% and JetBlue at 82.27%. American Airlines finished sixth at 80.10%, despite operating more flights than any other carrier in the regional ranking. The complete October results are shown below, including the proportion of scheduled flights completed by each airline.

Southwest finished 18 percentage points behind Delta and nearly seven points behind ninth-placed Air Canada. Its punctuality was also considerably below the 79.8% unweighted average across the 10 ranked airlines. Nevertheless, Southwest completed 99.5% of its scheduled flights, suggesting that its principal challenge was delays rather than cancellations.

Southwest's deteriorating performance coincides with some of the biggest changes in its history. The airline introduced checked-baggage fees in May last year, which coincided with a ten-point drop in on-time arrivals over a three-month period. Assigned seating and a new boarding process followed in January 2026, and over the past six months its on-time performance has averaged less than 70%.

Regular readers of Simple Flying know that the move to assigned seating was anything but smooth, and the airline continues to try iron out some of the challenges. This was likely compounded by charging for checked bags, as this encourages more passengers to bring carry-on luggage, increasing overhead-bin congestion and boarding times. CEO Bob Jordan acknowledged the airline's punctuality problems during the carrier's October 22 earnings call, despite praising its completion factor and baggage-handling performance:

"We've got a terrific completion factor, but OTP is not where we want it to be."

During the same call, COO Andrew Watterson explained that aircraft turnarounds were generally starting well, but the airline was losing valuable minutes toward the end of boarding. As a result, he outlined two procedural changes scheduled to begin in November, including how the airline handles bags checked at the departure gate, and allowing gate agents to use an iPad at the aircraft door to finalize the flight, rather than returning to the gate podium. More significant changes to crew reporting and operational schedules begin in January.

The airline recently changed its long-standing boarding process.

Cirium's airport rankings provide another perspective on operational performance. Six US airports featured among the world's 20 most punctual large airports in October, including four major Delta hubs. Minneapolis-St. Paul International Airport (MSP) led the American contingent with 87% on-time departures, placing eighth globally, followed by Washington Dulles International Airport (IAD) and Detroit Metropolitan Wayne County Airport (DTW). Unlike the airline rankings, these figures measure departures rather than arrivals.

Delta's hubs at Minneapolis, Detroit, Salt Lake City and Atlanta all featured prominently, contributing to the carrier's market-leading punctuality. None of Southwest's major operating bases, including Denver, Dallas Love Field, Chicago Midway and Baltimore/Washington, appeared in Cirium's rankings. With its operational overhaul continuing, the airline now faces mounting pressure to restore the reliability that helped establish its original reputation.

However, that doesn't mean the danger posed by Godzilla has been resolved.

Paying off the sequel's thematic arc of exploring the cycle of violence that causes warfare and bloodshed, Godzilla Minus Zero's climax leaves the door open for a larger plot that follows governments and militaries as they try to turn creatures like Godzilla into weapons. It's a compelling ending that never loses sight of what makes the film so thematically rich.

Godzilla Minus Zero closes out with the titular Kaiju as the undisputed king of the monsters, but with a very different type of target on his back, courtesy of the United States military. Initially, the battle between Godzilla and Ghidorah is meant to weaken the victor enough so that the American and Japanese forces could sweep in and take them down once and for all.

However, the American soldiers back off, implicitly revealing that the military wants to weaponize Murakami's Resonance Wave generator. This is a pointed thematic direction for the film, given Murakami's underlying subplot about wanting to do the same thing but as a means of getting revenge on the United States for the nuclear bombs that hit Hiroshima and Nagasaki.

The film ends with Godzilla making its way back to the ocean, firmly establishing itself as an engine of destruction on a global scale. The idea that governments and militaries would immediately try to weaponize them presents this world with a new version of the Cold War that could heat up very quickly. Noriko's status as the only survivor of the G-Cell infection from Godzilla Minus One means she's the one person capable of using the Resonance Wave generator to connect with (and effectively summon) Godzilla. It's also a good way to set up a more active adventure next time around, as a prospective sequel could follow the family on the move.

One of the biggest thematic throughlines of the film is the shadow of the atomic bomb. Murakami's family was killed in Hiroshima, leaving him reeling from a grief and fury that almost leads him to stage a calamitous rebuttal with Godzilla. It's the pursuit of an absolute power at the moral cost of countless lives that were and could have been.

It all makes Godzilla and the efforts to weaponize him an apt metaphor for the efforts to build upon the destructive power of the atomic bomb. Whether driven by national ambition or spiteful rage, the desire to have the biggest weapon just raises the risk that more innocent people will be caught in the crosshairs. There's a reason the film underscores the value of every person and is horrified at every death.

It's a key element of the DNA of the franchise, going all the way back to 1954's Godzilla. Famously, Godzilla was designed as a thematic reflection of the destructive power and mortal cost of the nuclear bomb. This makes its direct parallelism to Hiroshima, particularly as a movie openly reeling from America's actions, feel like the movie's most powerful political point.

As Nado pleads with Murakami before the final mission, it's on them to end the cycle of violence. While he could get revenge by bringing Godzilla to New York City, he would only be killing countless more and prompting more revenge. There's no way to escape the grief they suffer from but it's still on them to recognize the pattern and break it.

Godzilla Minus Zero builds on the survivor's guilt that pervaded the first film and takes it even further by highlighting the responsibility that the survivors take on in the world left behind. Much of the movie is focused on people reeling from trauma, either their experiences during the war or in the aftermath of Godzilla's last attack.

The heroes of the film are people like Shikishima, Noriko, and Nado, who recognize the price these kinds of events incur and refuse to let it be used again. They are traumatized, but focused on helping others and keeping that same pain from befalling others. That trauma can also spur on grimmer thoughts, like with Murakami, but it's possible to move beyond them. In fact, humanity must do so if it wants to thrive.

Godzilla Minus Zero doesn't hold back with its criticism of militarization in the nuclear age, the constant escalating and evolving danger represented by the ever-adaptable Godzilla and Ghidorah. The ending of Godzilla Minus Zero, with the survivors in Japan refusing to let the rest of the world suffer the same things they experienced, takes on a greater meaning as a result.

Few other streamers even come close to Apple TV’s science fiction catalog, so it’s quite the feat when a new sci-fi show on the platform marks itself out as a work of unique brilliance. That’s exactly what happened last November, when a mind-bending masterpiece like nothing we’d seen before arrived on our screens for the first time.

This series is surely the best new arrival on Apple TV in any genre since Severance’s first season in 2022. From the moment it dropped, it’s been hailed as one of the outstanding sci-fi shows of the 2020s. The show has already developed a devoted fanbase, who’ve been waiting with bated breath for updates about its second season.

Given that it debuted in 2025, viewers are naturally expecting Vince Gilligan’s Pluribus to return in 2027, following the two-year production cycle that’s been typical of prestige TV dramas across the past decade, particularly when following up their first season. Yet, the latest news from producers has effectively confirmed that this dystopian psychological thriller won’t be back next year.

When it premiered on Apple TV last November, Pluribus immediately broke an Apple TV record Severance had held for the previous three years, by achieving a perfect 100% Rotten Tomatoes score for its first two episodes. A new sci-fi rivalry was born in an instant. Now, the team behind Severance can celebrate their third season having a clear run at 2027.

Pluribus season 2 is currently being filmed, which gives fans even more time to unpick its first season, and come up with their own speculative theories about what’s going to happen next. The show’s premise is ostensibly simple, yet opens a Pandora's box of mysterious implications as terrifying as they are complex.

Longtime Vince Gilligan collaborator Rhea Seehorn plays protagonist Carol Sturka, who is seemingly immune to an alien virus that merges the consciousness of almost everyone on Earth into a single hive mind. Carol must uncover the truth about the virus, partly by connecting with the handful of other humans who’ve also seemingly avoided its effects.

Pluribus has finally revealed what the “Others” have been drinking from milk cartons, and the truth is more shocking than one could have imagined.

At the same time, she’s in a race against time, as the hive mind itself is looking for ways to ensnare her and the rest of the outsiders, absorbing them into its singular consciousness. This bizarre and deeply unsettling metaphor for herd mentality clearly draws on classic sci-fi shows like The Twilight Zone, The Prisoner, and The X-Files.

It’s little wonder, then, that it’s gone down a treat with fans of the genre and more casual viewers alike, with audiences spanning generations and cultures. Pluribus is one of the TV sci-fi events of the century so far, which is why we’re waiting with bated breath for confirmation about the release window of its second season.

A fresh update on the release timeline of Pluribus season 2 has essentially confirmed the fears of those who were worried about the lack of news before now. Sci-Fi Spiral writer Ava Harper officially revealed on X that shooting for the season began on November 1. What’s more, filming is due to wrap on June 14, 2027.

We should then expect at least a year of post-production work on the new episodes, in line with the post-production schedule for Pluribus season 1. Taking this work into account, the most optimistic estimates predict a summer 2028 release for season 2. That feels like a long time away, especially in light of Rhea Seehorn’s tantalizing tease about episode 1 of this new Pluribus season.

It should be worth the wait, of course, but Apple TV and Vince Gilligan are certainly testing the patience of their audience with this schedule. Pluribus season 3 will be out two years in the next decade at this rate, and could well be one of the sci-fi events of the 2030s.

I read with interest these comments from a journalist about the Newcastle United fans.

I only saw the articles today (Thursday 5 November 2026).

It was published on Tuesday, the day after the 1-0 home defeat to Everton.

It was a lengthy article by The Telegraph billed as an ‘Exclusive’, the headline declaring ‘Newcastle players feel hamstrung by poor recruitment’…

The main thrust of the article was that The Telegraph claimed they had sources giving them an inside track regarding certain Newcastle United players, that they weren’t happy with the summer recruitment, nor happy with the tactics used by Matthias Jaissle.

The article was credited to both Matt Law and Luke Edwards of The Telegraph.

I have no idea whether or not this is what certain Newcastle United players have been saying. I am assuming this is the truth and the newspaper had one or more credible source(s) for this.

What I did find unacceptable though, was something else in the article. Well a couple of things.

This was when backing up the unhappiness of the players angle, when talking about the fans.

I am sure this was just incompetence from Luke Edwards, rather than intentional untruths.

Talking about the Everton match he stated:

‘For the first time in years, you could still get tickets for the game on matchday. Newcastle fans are drained and deflated. They are starting to wonder whether this team, playing such ugly and disjointed long-ball football, is still worthy of their passionate and unflinching support.

We have not heard a Newcastle team booed and whistled at St James’ Park since the bleak, dark days of the Steve Bruce era.’

The Everton match was exactly the same as normal. All of the normal (non-hospitality) tickets for Newcastle United fans were as usual sold out for this match to members when the ballot sale and queue sale happened. The match by match tickets split over these two routes as is always the case. This happened weeks before the match.

What DOES happen ahead of each home game is that season ticket holders can decide to try and sell their tickets via the club’s official ticketing site to members. This means that even on a matchday there will usually be some tickets available, whether due to illness or whatever reasons, including fans simply deciding they don’t want to go. It obviously varies how many game to game.

Here on The Mag we highlighted how late last season more than a thousand of these tickets were getting put up for resale for certain matches by season ticket holders, such as the Brighton one. Many of them getting bought and/or season ticket holders ending up still going to the match if nobody bought their ticket online.

There has actually been at least one match where there WERE in the past tickets still on sale by the club when it got to matchday AND they didn’t sell out. This was the Champions League home game against Qarabag on 24 February 2026 when the official attendance was 50,068 after United won the first leg 5-1 and the ticket prices had been raised to unreasonable levels.

The idea that until Monday night a Newcastle United team (and manager) had never been booed by the fans since the days of Steve Bruce is laughable.

I don’t believe in booing the Newcastle United team and/or manager, well not since Steve Bruce anyway….

However, it simply isn’t true that this hasn’t happened before Everton on Monday.

Indeed, if you go back to 28 February 2026, both the Newcastle United team and manager were booed at half-time and full-time against…Everton. Eddie Howe’s side trailing 2-1 at HT and 3-2 at the end.

That came during a period of five defeats in six home Premier League matches. There were certainly plenty of boos for instance in reaction to the 3-2 defeat to Brentford, the 2-1 loss to Bournemouth AND the 2-1 defeat to Sunderland.

There were other previous instances as well, certainly the 4-1 home defeat to Bournemouth I recall in a previous season.

There are a lot of negatives at the moment but best to stick to the facts.

Thun doubled their lead in the 33rd minute through striker Brighton Labeau after more poor defending, this time from skipper Stephen Kingsley – who went off later with an injury – and then Furkan Dursun grabbed a third in the 56th minute before Jambos defender Stuart Findlay reduced the deficit in the 82nd minute with a header.

Hearts were left with one Conference League point out of nine following a 1-1 home draw against Nordsjaelland and a 2-0 defeat away to Trabzonspor.

Thick smoke from a pyro halted the game soon after kick-off but when play resumed after a lengthy delay – there were nine minutes added on at the end of the first half – Hearts winger Josh McPake drew a save from Niklas Steffen with an angled drive that lacked real power.

In the 20th minute McPake’s effort from 25 yards after he had drifted in from the left had the power but was beaten away by the Thun keeper.

There appeared to be nothing untoward on the horizon but then came a Hearts horror show.

There was no real pressure on the Jambos goal when a long ball came into the box but with Lucien Dahler at his shoulder, a simple pass back from McCart somehow evaded Hearts keeper Beau Reus and slipped into the corner of the net.

Thun’s second goal was also gift-wrapped.

The home side broke clear and with pace following a Heart set-piece and when Nils Reichmuth crossed into the box from the right, Kingsley stuck out a leg to clear but the ball went straight to Labeau who swept it into the net from 10 yards out.

Dursun was shown a yellow card for a forearm challenge into the face of Findlay, and it perhaps deserved more punishment.

The interval gave the Tynecastle side time to reset but three minutes after the break, Reus made a diving save from Dursun and an even better one from Reichmuth moments later.

However, the keeper was beaten once again when Dursun got his toe to a Reichmuth cross.

On the hour mark, Nico Maier raced clear of the Hearts defence but although his chip beat Reus, it sped wide of the target.

The night got worse for the rattled visitors as injured Kingsley was replaced by 19-year-old Matthew Gillies, called back from a loan spell at Stenhousemuir.

Malachi Fagan-Walcott’s header from a Jordi Altena free-kick was brilliantly saved by Steffen.

In the 78th minute Jan Bamert had the ball in the net after Steffen tipped a free-kick from substitute Nassim Zoukit on to the bar but was ruled offside, before Findlay headed in a consolation from another Altena free-kick.

The turning point that inspired Celtic to big European victory against Besiktas

📅 2026-11-05  |  Read Source →

On Guy Fawkes night, Celtic finally provided some European fireworks. Martin O'Neill's side got their Europa League campaign up and running with an enthralling 3-2 win over Besiktas.

Junior Olaitain put the visitors ahead in the 54th minute, scoring from the rebound of his own penalty kick that was initially saved by Sam Johnstone.

Celtic Park was engulfed by an all too familiar feeling before O'Neill introduced Callum Osmand and Camilo Duran in the 60th minute.

Osmand scored the equaliser seconds after coming on, with Duran latching on to the youngster's through ball to round Alex Nubel and put Celtic ahead with 12 minutes remaining.

Orkun Kokcu's pile-driver levelled things up for less than 120 seconds as Duran struck again in the 88th minute to give Celtic a priceless victory.

Here are the key talking points from Parkhead...

Celtic's Callum Osmand scored against Besiktas. (Image: Andrew Milligan/PA)

O’Neill made five changes from Sunday’s defeat at Falkirk, most notably drafting Jordan Lotomba in for his first start at right-back.

The Switzerland international had played just 31 minutes in green-and-white hoops before Besiktas, making his inclusion all the more surprising.

Lotomba was tasked with keeping Besiktas' left side quiet though former Arsenal attacker Leandro Trossard and Ernest Poku kept him well occupied.

The 28-year-old sought to maraud forward when he could, but once tripped up on his own feet when attempting a series of step-overs in the opposition third.

The concession of a second-half penalty compounded Lotomba's tough evening. He was withdrawn in the 60th minute for Colby Donovan.

Elsewhere, Kasper Hogh, James Forrest, and Sebastian Tounekti made up a second string frontline.

Duran, Celtic’s top scorer this season, dropped to the bench along with Haissem Hassan. But it wasn’t a completely rotated XI.

Regulars Kieran Tierney, Cameron Carter-Vickers, Liam Scales, and captain Callum McGregor all began, with Benjamin Nygren and Mika Baur also in from the off.

That said, it was the first time O’Neill hadn’t gone full strength in Europe this season, perhaps indicating his feelings at Celtic’s current plight.

There are dull halves of football, and then there are dull halves of football. Besiktas enjoyed the majority of possession (61 per cent) in the opening 45 minutes without mustering many opportunities.

Celtic's sound structure limited the visitors' attacking play. Carter-Vickers defended ably but O'Neill's side struggled to get forward themselves.

By the break, Celtic had just one shot on goal, two touches in the opposition box, and 73 per cent pass accuracy, compared to around 88 per cent in their first three Europa League defeats.

Yet again, their inability to progress play through the lines was apparent. Celtic resorted to going long on a number of occasions. Hogh failed to make it stick and Tounekti struggled greatly.

Besiktas were quite content to pop the ball around, without taking any major risks. You still felt the Turks had a few more gears to move through.

Celtic shouldn't have needed a second-half wake-up call but just seconds after the interval, Trossard fired just shy of the left post.

Moments later, Johnstone's clearance ricocheted off the Belgian attacker and into Lotomba's right-back area. The Celtic defender slid needlessly in on Poku and referee Sascha Stegemann pointed to the spot without hesitation.

Olaitan stepped up and Johnstone made a heroic save that flew into the smoke-filled Glasgow sky, only for the initial taker to score on the rebound.

Just like Johnstone, Lotomba almost redeemed himself in the following seconds, driving forward from his defensive position before tumbling over the ball when attempting to shoot.

But Besiktas weren't ahead for long. O'Neill looked to his bench for fresh impetus on the hour mark and within just a minute of coming on, Osmand scored.

McGregor latched brilliantly on to a loose midfield pass and cut back for the substitute, who scored with practically his first touch.

Celtic Park had all of a sudden rediscovered its European voice.

For the goal, McGregor looked like his old self, driving forward from the engine room to kickstart a promising attack and Osmand provided the killer touch with his first European goal.

If O'Neill's plan was to wait until the game opened up in the second half and then call on the cavalry, it worked a treat.

Just 16 minutes after Osmand drew Celtic level with a fine strike of his own, he turned provider, playing a slide-rule pass in behind Besiktas' defence for Duran.

The Colombian got there ahead of Nubel and slotted beyond the goal line to turn the game on its head.

Duran didn't have to wait long to make his presence felt again, once more beating Nubel, this time from close range, after brilliantly controlling Tierney's mishit shot. It came seconds after Kokcu's leveller.

A true difference-maker, Duran is now up to 13 goals for the season. His impact could not have been greater here.

Well, who saw that coming? After four matchdays, Celtic are finally up and running in the Europa League. It really was now or never for O'Neill's charges, who have given their chances of progression to the knockout stages fresh hope with an unlikely victory.

Beyond their run of six defeats in ten leading into Thursday night, what made the win even more unexpected was the fact Besiktas are the best side they've played at home in the league phase so far.

Celtic struggled greatly against both Ferencvaros and Celta Vigo on their own turf, flailing whenever faced with adversity. They went down meekly in Lisbon too, losing 4-1 to Benfica last month.

No one should get carried away based on one result. Celtic still need somewhere in the region of six to eight points to finish in the top 24 and three out of their four remaining fixtures are away.

The next comes in Leiria against Portuguese second tier side Torreense after the international break. They could realistically gain three more points there before a trip to face Omonia Nicosia in December. Ties against Marseille and Union St-Gilloise lie ahead in January.

O'Neill will take great satisfaction from a European result that's long evaded his team. Time will define its influence on Celtic's wider Europa League picture.

How much Celtic earned in Europa League win over Besiktas

📅 2026-11-05  |  Read Source →

Celtic claimed their first win in the Europa League this season as they beat Besiktas 3-2 at Parkhead.

Martin O'Neill's side came from behind to take their first three points in the competition after three losses up to that point.

And in bagging their first win, Celtic also earned a bonus payment from UEFA for achieving a win in the league phase of the competition.

UEFA offer bonuses to clubs for wins or draws in the league phase of the competition.

By beating Besiktas, Celtic banked €450k or £375k in prize money from UEFA.

The fee comes on top of the €4.31m paid to every club involved in the league phase of the Europa League this season.

Qualification for the knockout round would mean a further €300k from the UEFA prize pot, with Celtic guaranteed a payment for their final position in the league phase, regardless of whether they advance.

The bottom club in the league phase will earn €75k, with that figure increased by a further €75k for every position higher a club finishes in the table.

− victory in the league phase €450k, draw in league phase €150k

− qualification for the knockout round play-offs: €300k per club

− qualification for the round of 16: €1.75m per club

− qualification for the quarter-finals: €2.5m per club

− qualification for the semi-finals: €4.2m per club

− qualification for the final: €7m per club

The UEL winners can expect to receive an additional €6m
