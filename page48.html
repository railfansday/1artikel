Barry Hits Double As Everton Win Moyes' Landmark Match

📅 2026-11-07  |  Read Source →

Thierno Barry scored two and created the other as Everton made it three wins on the spin to beat Coventry 3-1 in David Moyes' record-equalling match as Blues manager.

The Toffees had to recover from Yann Gboho's 52nd-minute opener as Barry headed home Vitalii Mykolenko's cross four minutes later, before taking his goal tally to 10 for  the season when he bundled in Tyrique George's centre just past the hour mark.

Three points – which lifted Everton into fifth place in the Premier League table ahead of this weekend's round of matches – were then secured against Frank Lampard's top-flight newcomers nine minutes from time when Barry slid in Kiernan Dewsbury-Hall to blast past Carl Rushworth.

That ensured the Blues went into the upcoming international break buoyant as Moyes tasted victory in his 592nd match in charge of the Club, levelling the previous record set by Harry Catterick.

Moyes made one change from the team that triumphed 1-0 at Newcastle four days earlier, Tyler Dibling getting the nod at right wing as Merlin Röhl dropped to right-back in place of Jake O'Brien.

After a bright opening from the visitors, Everton soon found a foothold in the game to carve out three opportunities in quick succession. A slick one-two between George and Dewsbury-Hall on nine minutes – that started with a lovely backheel from the winger – allowed George to play Barry through on the right side of the penalty area. The Frenchman did not get the ball set with his first touch, however, as he was pushed wide and unable to get a clean strike on goal.

Seconds later, Coventry made a hash of a defensive clearance, allowing Mykolenko – still up from the previous attack – a sight at goal as he drove into the arms of keeper Carl Rushworth from just inside the area.

The best chance so far soon followed as Branthwaite won a recycled header from Dewsbury-Hall's free-kick, leading to a scramble in the box involving Barry and Armstrong that ended with the Academy graduate trying to nod home his maiden Blues goal before Coventry cleared.

Everton couldn't build on that momentum, though, and Coventry were the next to threaten as the first half wore on, Pickford needing to save Sakamoto's shot with his legs after the Japanese winger had been slid through, before Jack Rudoni whistled a low angled drive inches beyond the left-hand post.

The Blues could count themselves fortunate not to go in behind at half-time but it wasn't long before Coventry were in front after the restart.

Lampard's side were allowed too much space to move in from the right channel as Gboho played a one-two with Sakamoto and knocked a close-range effort through Branthwaite's legs and past Pickford.

It left Everton needing a strong response – and they duly delivered with a quickfire double from that man Barry.

Just four minutes after Coventry's opener, the striker got on the end of Mykolenko's pinpoint left-wing cross to nod into the bottom right-hand corner.

And Barry made it double figures for the season just after the hour mark as George turned provider from the left flank this time, floating a ball to the back post for the Frenchman to somehow squeeze over the line off his midriff.

Barry may have been making headlines for his goalscoring this season but he showed the quality in his all-round game to make a fine Everton turnaround complete with nine minutes to go.

Collecting possession just inside Coventry's half, he spun and threaded a beauty of a through ball down the left channel for Dewsbury-Hall to chase. There was still plenty of work for the Toffees' No.8 to do but Dewsbury-Hall – so unlucky not to make the England squad announced earlier in the day – took his chance to smash a fierce finish past Rushworth at his near post.

Former Australia opener Usman Khawaja has claimed that the side's entire dressing room knew about the ball-tampering incident in Cape Town in 2018.

That declaration, made by Khawaja in the Sydney Morning Herald ahead of the release of his book 'Sabr', is at odds with Cricket Australia's assertion that only Steve Smith, David Warner and Cameron Bancroft were aware of the plot to use sandpaper on the ball in the third Test against South Africa eight years ago.

Khawaja, who retired from international duty after Australia's 4-1 Ashes thrashing of England last winter, said: "It was all of us. Not the batters, not the bowlers, not the staff. All of us."

Captain-at-the-time Smith, Bancroft and Warner, who was found to have been the architect of the 'Sandpapergate' plot, all received bans after the incident, with Smith and Warner suspended for one year and Bancroft handed a nine-month sanction.

Bowlers Pat Cummins, Mitchell Starc, Josh Hazlewood and Nathan Lyon issued a joint statement in 2021 insisting they had no idea about the ball-tampering.

That came after Bancroft - who had aimed to conceal the sandpaper down his trousers during the Test in question - replied "I think it's probably self-explanatory" when asked in a newspaper interview whether the bowling attack knew about the scandal.

Cummins, Starc and Hazlewood have all been rested from this month's white-ball series at home to England while Lyon is not in the limited-overs set-up, so the quartet may not be quizzed on Khawaja's claims just yet.

Khawaja wrote in his book that ball-tampering plans were first discussed in the second Test of the 2018 series in Port Elizabeth, now Gqeberha.

He continued: "In an ideal world, I don't want to do this.

"I don't want to be the one who comes out and talks about the truth. The issue is that I genuinely thought after eight years the truth would have been out by now.

"Maybe I thought Davey [Warner] would have exposed it, or someone else would have talked about it more.

"There has been a great deal of speculation in the eight years since the 2018 Cape Town Test, including extensive media coverage throughout last month's series in South Africa.

"During this time there has been the ongoing opportunity to present any relevant information, and this remains the case.

"All relevant information presented to CA to date has been properly considered and we remain comfortable with the findings of our integrity process."

"At the end of the day, I think it's the right thing to do, particularly for those three guys. And I think most of the guys in the team, at some level, will be relieved we can finally move on.

"Even in the change rooms when I was talking to them after the game. I said: 'Take this as a team. We did this as a team. We knew what was going on. We have to take it as a team'.

"I was always of that stance. Obviously, it didn't work that way, and those three guys got crucified.

"I've seen Davey over the last eight years just get nailed bad over and over again. It's taken a big man not to say anything and just to stay quiet. He's just copped it and copped it and copped it.

'A beautiful story' as Bellingham brothers called up

Jude and Jobe Bellingham could be the first brothers to play together for England since Gary and Phil Neville 19 years ago

It may be a surprise to some but Jobe Bellingham's call-up to the England senior side has been a long time coming.

The 21-year-old's career path has been very similar to that of his 23-year-old brother Jude - England's star player at the World Cup and one of Real Madrid's best players. Both were stars of the Birmingham City academy before big-money moves to the German Bundesliga and England call-ups.

Jobe's debut for Birmingham came at the age of 16 years and 107 days - 69 days older than Jude when he made his first appearance for the Blues' senior side.

The central midfielder then moved to Sunderland and helped them win promotion to the Premier League before joining German side Borussia Dortmund at the age of 19 in a deal worth up to Â£31m.

Jude also moved to Dortmund when he was 17, before developing into one of the world's best players and joining Real Madrid.

Having one of the best midfielders in the world as your older brother comes with challenges and Jobe has consistently been compared to his sibling.

But the former Sunderland midfielder is his own player, wearing 'Jobe' on the back of his shirt instead of his family name. He said it was suggested to him by a former coach because "it shows that I'm going my own way".

England manager Thomas Tuchel told BBC Radio 5 Live it is "a beautiful story".

"They are both good for each other," he added. "My understanding is they are very close.

"It makes life easier for Jobe to come into camp and know his brother is one of the key players in our team. So, that will hopefully help him, but don't forget he is here on his own merits, and he is not here because of his brother."

Jobe is a different type of player to Jude, sitting deeper in the midfield, but is as physically imposing while still being good on the ball.

He is the current captain of the England Under-21 side, and Tuchel said Young Lions head coach Lee Carsley had been pushing for some time for the younger Bellingham to get senior recognition.

"He's the standout player," said Tuchel. "He's the captain, so the door should always be open for the pathway, always be open for special performances over a long period of time.

"That's what Jobe has done on top of it. He found his way, he found his rhythm with Dortmund. He's had an excellent start to his season, is a regular starter and influential."

Thomas Tuchel on Jobe Bellingham's England call-up

England host Croatia at Wembley on Thursday, 12 November before travelling to Madrid to take on Spain on 15 November in this next international break.

With Arsenal's Myles Lewis-Skelly suspended for the first game, and Bournemouth's Alex Scott out injured after shining on his debut in last month's 7-0 win over Croatia, Tuchel said a natural "opportunity" for Jobe had opened up.

"He deserves to make an impression for us," said the German in his news conference after naming the squad.

"It's an incredible achievement for the family and for the brothers. I saw his excitement straight away and his smile.

"He has an open and calm personality. He's one of five players for the central midfield.

"He's a more defensive player than Jude. So strong on the ball, good physique, a big body, a very physical player and can keep possession.

"It's now up to him, but he's excited and happy and eager to impress, and that's the best foundation to have a good camp for him."

If they get on the pitch together, Jude and Jobe won't be the first brothers to represent the Three Lions.

It has been 19 years since Gary and Phil Neville, who both played for Manchester United, last played together for England, and 30 years since they started.

They first played together for England in 1996 against China, becoming the first siblings to do so since Bobby and Jack Charlton 26 years earlier.

Jack and Bobby were both part of England's 1966 World Cup-winning squad.

Bobby was the younger of the two, but made his Three Lions debut first. He went on to win 106 international caps.

Jack, who was not called into the England team until days before his 30th birthday, won 35 caps.

More recently, brothers Nathaniel and Trevoh Chalobah have both played for England, but never alongside each other.

Nathaniel made his solitary appearance as an injury-time substitute in a 3-2 away win against Spain in a 2018 Nations League game.

His younger brother Trevoh, currently a Como defender, made the first of his three international appearances against Senegal in June 2025 and was in England's 2026 World Cup squad.

Bobby and Jack Charlton were both in the England squad that lifted the Jules Rimet Trophy at Wembley.

The Chalobah brothers never played together for England, but both featured for the senior side.

Jobe Bellingham has made 14 appearances for Dortmund this season

Jobe has shown remarkable progress since last winter. In the first couple of months, following his move from Sunderland to Borussia Dortmund, he seemed timid, as if he was just trying to avoid making any mistakes. His passes rarely, if ever, caused trouble for opposing defences.

Even Dortmund manager Niko Kovac said at one point that he was surprised how long it might take him to get comfortable in the Bundesliga. But hard work in the gym to build up muscle mass in order to become more explosive and the fact that he received plenty of chances in Dortmund's line-up has helped his development.

In the latter stages of last season, his decision-making was much faster and his intensity seemed higher. Still, Dortmund decided to sign Dutch midfielder Joey Veerman, a specialist at making line-breaking passes, in August.

It meant Jobe's place in the starting XI was far from secure at the beginning of this season.

In recent games, Kovac has employed Bellingham in a more advanced role, as one of the two number 10s behind the striker. In this role, Bellingham drove into the final third with pace and showed intensity during pressing sequences.

His performance in the 2-2 draw against Bayern Munich in the Bundesliga last Saturday was arguably his best since his arrival in Germany.

Palmer's fall from grace with England can be illustrated by the fact he was regarded as part of a golden future when he scored a superb goal in a losing cause against Spain in the Euro 2024 final in Berlin.

He was a contentious omission from England's squad for the World Cup last summer, but Tuchel's selection was shaped at that point by Palmer's indifferent season at club level with Chelsea - along with injury problems.

This latest setback encapsulates an England career that has been stop-start since making his debut under Tuchel's predecessor, Sir Gareth Southgate, in a 2-0 win against Malta in a European Championship qualifier at Wembley in November 2023.

He has won 14 England caps, with two goals and one assist, his last appearance coming in the number 10 role in the disappointing 1-0 defeat by Japan at Wembley in March, when he was substituted after less than an hour.

More tellingly, perhaps, is that he has only figured in three of Tuchel's 26 games since he succeeded Southgate.

Far from being a centrepiece of England's plans, Palmer is now on the outside looking in, with seemingly no immediate prospect of forcing his way back.

'Palmer misses too many opportunities to impress' - Tuchel

Palmer was offered reassuring words of sorts by Tuchel, but his predicament is now clear. He has allowed others to form an orderly queue in front of him in England's pecking order. They are now Tuchel's priority, not Palmer.

Tuchel said: "We have enough number 10s and we stick to the guys who were in the previous camp, because we were happy with the two and a half weeks we had together.

"I guess everyone is desperate to play with England and Cole is the same. He misses too many opportunities to impress. It was a long camp but the players in that camp brought something extraordinary."

"It's not personal. We will never assume Cole is in the mindset never to come to the camp, but we stick to the guys who were in camp last time. It was excellent. The impact of all the players was immense. We have Josh King as a new player, who can play as a 10 and on both wings."

He added: "Cole is always in our mindset but the competition is on."

There may be additional pain, and perhaps a measure of puzzlement, for Palmer in that Tuchel has not just decided to stick with the players from the last camp - calling up Fulham's King and, the major incoming surprise, 21-year-old Jobe Bellingham from Borussia Dortmund.

Palmer said he had played for Chelsea with "discomfort and pain" before the last round of England fixtures, which included a 3-2 loss to Spain at Wembley, a win away to the Czech Republic, a Wembley victory in that return fixture and a 7-0 rout of Croatia in Rijeka.

And Chelsea manager Xabi Alonso has also insisted Palmer was "struggling" in Chelsea's 3-0 defeat at Brentford before the international break.

It is believed Palmer's desire to play for England remains as strong as ever, with the fact that his state of fitness was checked out before Tuchel named his squad taken as a sign he remains in his thoughts.

Alonso confirmed England had made checks on Palmer, adding: "When a player is fully fit he is selectable. When he is not fit we give the national team the reasonable information to decide together. Sometimes it's not good for the player."

Chelsea's boss said he has not spoken to Palmer about his England exclusion and has no doubts about his character, although the pair had talks about his recent sending off in the 2-0 home loss to Tottenham Hotspur.

He said: "We have not talked about England. We are focused on Chelsea things, how to help him, how he can become more and more influential. These are our topics to talk.

"We speak about Chelsea. We have enough on our plate. My impact area is my control area and what we talk about.

"I am very happy with his quality, with his attitude and the impact he is having with the rest of the team."

Even before Palmer withdrew last time out, Tuchel had previously said he had a "point to prove" and needed "to step up" to "keep his shirt and justify the nomination".

Is Tuchel convinced Palmer has the motivation to play for England? Does his "trust" lie elsewhere?

He certainly has options in the position Palmer would occupy, with Real Madrid's Jude Bellingham his world class "number 10" and another Chelsea player, Morgan Rogers, an outstanding alternative.

Morgan Gibbs-White, Eberechi Eze and now 19-year-old King, who has won his first England senior call-up, have been selected ahead of Palmer, with Everton's Kiernan Dewsbury-Hall also given a glowing reference from Tuchel, despite not making the squad.

Alonso praises Palmer's quality and attitude

Palmer has made a good start to the season with Chelsea. The penalty he scored in the 2-0 win against Manchester United was his 50th league goal for the club in only 105 games.

This, however, has cut no ice with Tuchel, who has not just relegated him down the list of players included in England's squad, but out of his plans completely for now.

Tuchel has never been shy of taking hard-nosed decisions without a backward glance, such as excluding Jude Bellingham from England's squad for games against Latvia and Wales in October 2025, despite the player's insistence he was keen to be involved after recovering from shoulder surgery.

Real Madrid's Trent Alexander-Arnold is only just being rehabilitated after a long exile, including exclusion from the World Cup squad, with Tuchel making his reservations about his defensive deficiencies plain.

Palmer is the latest high-profile player who now has to prove his worth to Tuchel â and, given the head coach's words and his obvious faith and trust in those he has selected ahead of him, he could face a long road to England redemption.

He faces an anxious wait to see if he can persuade Tuchel he deserves a place in England's next squad, which will not be named until March 2027.

And, as he has found, with Tuchel there are no guarantees.

Lampard feels hair pull should have led to Coventry penalty

📅 2026-11-07  |  Read Source →

Barry scores twice as Everton fight back to beat Coventry

Coventry boss Frank Lampard questioned why his side were not given a penalty after a trio of decisions went against them in defeat at Everton.

Thierno Barry's double and Kiernan Dewsbury-Hall's late goal helped the hosts come from behind to win 3-1 on Friday.

Yann Gboho gave Coventry the lead after the break, yet the visitors felt they should have had a first-half spot-kick.

James Tarkowski tugged Caleb Yirenkyi's hair while jumping for a ball, with referee Stuart Attwell dismissing the Sky Blues' claims.

The rules have been relaxed around hair pulling this season and it does not automatically mean a foul or a red card, with excessive force and intent also being taken into consideration - but former Everton boss Lampard believes the officials got it wrong.

He said: "They're saying it's not enough of a pull and I think it's a hard area to be in. What is a hair pull? You've decided it is but it's not enough.

"I have looked at them, sometimes with emotions in the moment you can't be clear on the sidelines.

"I think the rules are fine. It's not a red card. I kind of get that because I think in a game of football there's a lot of stuff happens that's not absolutely dramatic.

"You know they can happen but I think if the the rule remains that it's a foul so I felt it was a foul."

Lampard also felt Taiwo Awoniyi was fouled by Vitalii Mykolenko before Coventry went ahead, although replays showed it was outside the box, and Barry handled the ball before his second goal - although video assistant referee (VAR) Timothy Wood ruled it was not a handball offence.

"Taiwo for me on the line of the box is a penalty. You've got to give the foul in real time anyway, the ref did let it go, and I felt it was a penalty," Lampard added, with defeat leaving Coventry 16th in the Premier League.

"The Barry one is handball, I've heard they are saying it didn't touch his hand but I've seen an angle where he has flicked it in with the final touch. VAR is there for that.

"I can't sit here and say I know everything and they should give all three of them, if one or two of them don't go our way, but they would clearly change the game."

Everton boss David Moyes denied Barry handled the ball and also felt the hair pulling rule needs addressing.

After his team moved fifth, the Scot said: "We've had plenty [of decisions] which have gone against us. It certainly doesn't hit his hand.

"The hair pull rule has changed so it would be a booking if it was but I still think it's ridiculous that rule is in the game. I can't understand why they don't fix this completely because I don't see it as a good rule at all."

Three big VAR calls, and they all went against Coventry. Lampard must have walked under a ladder earlier today.

There is a strong argument that the Sky Blues should have been awarded a penalty for the Tarkowski hair pull

The law was relaxed at the start of this season to say not all hair pulling should be a red card, after three players were sent off last season.

The idea is that not all contact with the hair should be an offence, and it needs to be an act with excessive force for a player to be sent off.

But, for a penalty, the actions of Tarkowski only need to be a foul, not violent conduct. If accidental, it should not be a spot-kick.

Did the Wood, the VAR, concentrate too much on a possible handball by Tarkowski within the move?

It looked like there was a movement of Tarkowski's arm to pull the hair which should have led to a VAR intervention. Coventry probably should have received a penalty.

Then early in the second half came a possible penalty as Mykolenko clipped Awoniyi.

The camera positioned level with the edge of the penalty area did seem to show the contact was outside the area, and the VAR cannot intervene to award a free-kick.

It might have been a soft VAR intervention even if the potential foul was inside the box.

Perhaps most controversially was Barry's second goal, with the ball appearing to touch the striker's arm before it went into the goal.

We have seen this kind of situation a few times this season, most notably when Nottingham Forest had a goal against Tottenham disallowed.

This time, the VAR decided he did not have the necessary conclusive proof to be sure the ball touched the hand.

Yet it does seem like there is a change of direction after Barry's hand touches the ball, and that was the reason it goes into the goal. It looked like handball should have been given on video review although others might argue that ruling it out would have been too forensic.

Three decisions, and Coventry can feel aggrieved about two of them.

Circulating concentrations of low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) and triglyceride-rich lipoproteins are leading risk factors for atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease1. Despite several approved lipid-lowering medications, most patients fail to achieve target lipid levels under the chronic care treatment model2,3. Here, we describe a potential single-course treatment in which an infusion of guide RNA and mRNA encoding a CRISPR adenine base editor—encapsulated within a GalNAc–lipid nanoparticle—leads to durable, liver-selective inactivation of angiopoietin-like 3 (ANGPTL3), a known driver of increased LDL-C and triglyceride concentrations. Analyses in human liver cells in vitro confirmed that the base editor introduces a DNA base-pair change at the target ANGPTL3 locus with no detectable off-target editing at any of approximately 3000 candidate sites or treatment-related structural variants. Targeted ANGPTL3 inactivation in nonhuman primates was well-tolerated and led to a > 90% mean reduction in circulating ANGPTL3 protein, with durability confirmed out to 3 years following a single dose. In mouse and nonhuman primate models of hyperlipidemia, gene inactivation was associated with reductions of approximately 40 to 50% in LDL-C and 50 to 70% in triglycerides. These results provided the scientific foundation for initiation of an ongoing first-in-human clinical trial in patients with refractory hyperlipidemia syndromes.

Archaeologists unearthed a Pompeii cupboard containing 38 coins and a bag of childhood charms that were buried in the 79 AD eruption

A Pompeii cupboard buried in 79 AD held 38 bronze coins, 36 from Nero's reign, 24 childhood charms in a cloth bag and, nearby, two young wood mice.

In a back room of House IX 11, 7 on Via dell’Abbondanza, archaeologists found the imprint of a wooden Pompeii cupboard in the volcanic ash.

The Pompeii Archaeological Park says it was never reopened after Mount Vesuvius erupted in 79 AD. Inside were 38 bronze coins in a basket, 24 small charms in a cloth bag, tweezers, a lamp and pots.

The park put out the news on October 29, 2026, with a 37-page report by director Gabriel Zuchtriegel and six colleagues. They do not know who lived there or what happened to them.

The front of the house was dug in 1912. Inscriptions around the door gave the name Cornelius Maximus. They can no longer be seen, and the report calls the name very doubtful.

The back rooms were reached only now, in the last phase of a project on the neighboring Insula dei Casti Amanti, the city block named for the House of the Chaste Lovers.

The goal was to make the slope safe along the alley between blocks 11 and 12, so old metal scaffolding could come down.

The top layer was modern fill with plastic and bits of metal. Under it the team found a toilet built into a wall niche in room D. Room C held a satyr-face roof tile and a bronze pot.

Room B, about 14 by 12.6 feet (4.3 by 3.85 meters), was probably a dining room. Its yellow walls show birds, panthers, a peacock and dolphins. Domenico Esposito, of the Freie Universität Berlin, put them down to the painters of Via di Castricio, a known workshop.

Building or repair work was going on in the house when the volcano erupted, the report says. Upper floors rarely survive at Pompeii, and researchers have used staircases that lead nowhere in the House of Thiasus to work out what stood above.

Two tunnels had been cut into these rooms after the eruption, at a date the report cannot fix. The second, about 3.6 feet (1.1 meters) wide, came from the west and ended at the east wall of room B.

It wrecked the fill in the middle of the room but left the ash along the sides. In that ash, the team found the shape of a big cupboard in the southeast corner.

It had two parts, each about 6.6 feet (2 meters) long. One was about 3 feet (0.9 meters) deep and the other 2.3 feet (0.7 meters). Two iron pieces fixed it to the floor.

The roof came down and broke the top of the cupboard. Then a pyroclastic flow, a fast current of hot ash and gas, came in from the northwest and filled it. More than half of its height survived.

The cupboard held five jugs, flasks, cooking pots, a glass plate, a bronze lamp and an incense burner. A thin cup had a pine-cone-scale pattern. There was also a glass ointment bottle, bronze tweezers and a slate palette for grinding makeup.

In the south part was a small wooden box with a bronze catch. It held eight lead weights, six with iron handles, and the authors say these may have been used for trade.

About 8 inches (0.2 meters) from the box lay a lump of 38 bronze coins. They had been kept in a little basket of woven plant fiber. Only part of its bottom, about 4.7 inches (0.12 meters), was left.

Two coins were old and worn, one from Ebusus, now Ibiza, and one a sestertius of Claudius struck in the name of Germanicus.

The other 36 were struck under Nero between 64 and 67 AD: 26 asses, seven dupondii and three sestertii, all Roman coin denominations. Twenty-one are from 65 AD.

The report calls it a small reserve, maybe for day-to-day spending. The team did not harden the basket with consolidant, so other tests could still be done. Archaeobotanist Chiara Comegna looked at it under a stereo microscope in the park’s lab.

About 2 feet (0.6 meters) from the east edge, along the inner south wall, the team found 24 tiny objects in faience, bone, glass paste and rock crystal. Each had a hole for a string.

There are the gods Bes and Mercury, hands, feet, pine cones or grapes, beads, a knucklebone and a winged shoe.

Romans called these crepundia, from a Latin word for rattling. Children wore them as protection, and adults could hear them moving about. Some earlier finds like these were taken for magic kit.

The bag sat on a middle shelf. A post about 6 inches (0.15 meters) thick fell on that shelf, the shelf tilted, and the bag slid to the back and tore. Under the microscope the cloth shows a regular weave at about 0.04 inches (1 millimeter).

Adults sometimes kept these necklaces all their lives, and at the Porta Nocera cemetery they were put in graves. The park notes that many Romans lived far from their parents and childhood homes.

Zuchtriegel said the find lets the team read “the different traces of a life” together: “personal habits, work, memories.”

In small bedroom B1 next door, seven post holes about 6.6 feet (2 meters) up the walls showed where a wooden loft had been. On it sat a North African olive oil amphora, six lids, three flasks, two pots and a big storage jar.

The bottom of the broken amphora was sieved and gave 117 tiny bones. Zooarchaeologist Younes Naime put them in the genus Apodemus, most likely the wood mouse. Naime counted two animals at the very least. Neither had finished growing.

Wood mice normally live in fields and gardens, the park says, but they slip into attics and food stores whenever they smell a meal. The house mouse is a different species, and it has followed people around since before farming began.

Back in 2023, the park described two mice and a young black rat found in the slave quarters of the Civita Giuliana villa. Rodent bones have also come out of the old sewers of Herculaneum.

Did the people of the house die in the eruption? Or did they run, and never make it back for their things? The report admits the evidence cannot settle it, and that is fair.

The press release mentioned Tacitus, who wrote that property left without heirs was put to use after the disaster. The report itself plays that down.

That link needs more study before anyone ties it to this house, and wall paintings alone say little about how rich a family was.

DNA tests on Pompeii’s victims have shown how wrong old guesses about sex and family can be. Other finds have pointed to the public role of women in the city.

“We cannot reconstruct with certainty the identity of whoever lived in the house,” Zuchtriegel said. Still to come: a textile specialist for the cloth samples, and more digging along the north and south walls of room B, which are only partly cleared.

Forget 'Se7en,' Jake Gyllenhaal's Apple TV Thriller 'Prisoners' Is Even Better

📅 2026-11-07  |  Read Source →

A 150-minute-plus, dark-as-night kidnapping thriller isn't an instant recipe for box office success, yet Denis Villeneuve's 2013 film Prisoners handily made almost three times its budget back, while also landing on numerous year-end Top 10 lists. And the sprawling, dour crime film is still proving to have legs, as it's currently rocketing up the charts on Apple TV.

While Prisoners doesn't have any actual supernatural elements, Villeneuve and master cinematographer Roger Deakins (Blade Runner 2049, The Assassination of Jesse James by the Coward Robert Ford) conjure such an enveloping atmosphere of menace that when villainous characters who may actually be related to a Satanic cult are introduced, it doesn't come as much of a shock at all. The film is long, unpleasantly themed, and generally hopeless. It's also a modern masterpiece, and currently receiving its due ahead of Villeneuve's eagerly anticipated Dune Part Three.

The story of Prisoners actually dates back to 2009, when Aaron Guzikowski penned a script based on his own short story, making that year's Black List. It was shopped around to stars and directors, reportedly including Leonardo DiCaprio and Antoine Fuqua, before landing in the capable hands of Denis Villeneuve. Fresh off the critically acclaimed (and extremely dark) dramatic thrillers Polytechnique and Incendies, Villeneuve proved the perfect match for the story of suburban Connecticut dad Keller Dover (Hugh Jackman), whose young daughter, along with his neighbor's child, vanishes into thin air on a chilly Thanksgiving. While Jake Gyllenhaal's Detective Loki works the case, Dover takes matters into his own hands.

Prisoners takes on an even darker tone once Dover becomes convinced that a young man with an intellectual disability, played masterfully by Paul Dano, is the culprit and takes matters into his own hands. Torture, amateur investigation, false leads, and an apparent occult kidnapping conspiracy dating back decades take over the final half, as the film keeps viewers' hearts pounding for over two and a half hours.

The story of Prisoners is fairly standard kidnapping thriller material (until it takes some dark and, arguably, unnecessary turns toward the occult and conspiratorial), but it's elevated by the sheer mood and craft that Villeneuve and Deakins bring to its world. The opening disappearance sequence is a perfect model of grey, chilly suburban banality that's nevertheless suffused with menace. Deakins lights abandoned buildings, streetlights, and parking lots like they're nestled somewhere within one of Dante's seven layers of hell. Lighting aside, Deakins' still, observational frames at time evoke another all-timer of a thriller featuring Gyllenhaal, Zodiac.

And frankly, despite its rather conventional story beats, Prisoners is rivaled by precious few American thrillers of the past several decades, including Zodiac, The Silence of the Lambs, and Se7en, a film from which it apparently borrows much of its hopeless, Satanic atmosphere. Jackman's role has been turned into a meme by many at this point, but his anguished father is actually astonishingly visceral work. In the film's famous torture sequence, in which Dover tries in vain to get information out of Dano's intellectually disabled suspect, Jackman reportedly improvised a few brutal swings of his hammer — with one landing so close to Dano's head it legitimately terrified the actor and caused him to cower to the floor.

Standing proudly alongside Se7en and The Silence of the Lambs as a potboiler elevated to high art through the magic of cinematic craftsmanship, Prisoners remains a compelling rewatch today. It's no wonder audiences are flocking to it on Apple TV.
