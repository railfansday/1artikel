Il Manchester United dopo 13 anni senza Sir Alex Ferguson non ha ancora imparato la lezione. Non solo: dopo 10 allenatori cambiati nello stesso arco temporale, se ne è aggiunto un altro all'elenco di "epurazione" apportata. Parliamo di Michael Carrick, colui che fino a sei mesi fa veniva osannato dai tifosi dei Red Devils e incensato dal club di Old Trafford, passando da tecnico ad interim ad allenatore con contratto firmato fino al 2028. Questo in seguito a una striscia di 12 vittorie su 17 partite per chiudere la scorsa stagione in bellezza, con qualificazione in Champions League assente dal 2023/24. Adesso, però, tutto è drasticamente cambiato.

Un declino sorprendente, fin dalla prima giornata inaugurale di Premier League contro il neopromosso Hull City (0-2) di agosto, che ha dato il via a un misero inizio di stagione. Una sola vittoria in campionato e appena 3 in tutte le competizioni (Champions League inclusa) su 15 gare disputate, equivalsi al 18° posto in classifica in Premier. Come se non bastasse, le tattiche di mister Carrick sono state messe in discussione dal capitano Bruno Fernandes, specialmente dopo la disfatta per 1-4 contro il Como di Cesc Fabregas. Vicino all'esonero dopo la sconfitta contro il Bournemouth il mese scorso, l'ultima partita in carica dell'ex iconico centrocampista inglese sulla panchina dei Red Devils è stata un pareggio rocambolesco per 4-4 con l'Aston Villa. Con questo risultato lo United si è ritrovato terzultimo. Di fronte alla prospettiva, un tempo impensabile, della retrocessione.

Al di là dei risultati e del piazzamento finale la scorsa stagione in Premier League - grande fetta decisionale per i vertici del club - quello che ha spinto i Red Devils a confermare Carrick questa estate è stato anche il comportamento adottato dall'allenatore inglese. Calmo, elegante e dannatamente attaccato alla causa Manchester United per il suo glorioso passato da calciatore lì. Inoltre, i commenti e le critiche da fuori non lo scalfivano, nemmeno di ex come Roy Keane, al contrario di altri predecessori. Questo è stato molto apprezzato dal vice direttore dell'area sportiva Jason Wilcox.

Durante l'estate sono state convocate diverse riunioni a Carrington per studiare il modo migliore per reintegrare Marcus Rashford. L'avvenuto ritorno del figliol prodigo dopo gli screzi avuti con l'ex Ruben Amorim si vocifera abbiano avuto un'influenza negativa sullo spogliatoio, rimasto confuso quando appena un anno prima era stato detto che l'attaccante inglese non fosse adatto all'ambiente che il club stava cercando di creare. Poi, la mancanza di fondi, quando Carrick sentiva di aver bisogno di più nuovi acquisti rispetto ai tre centrocampisti centrali (Andrey Santos, Youri Tielemans e Carlos Baleba) ma alla fine per rigidi vincoli di bilancio, visto che due estati fa i Red Devils hanno impiegato solamente 76,5 milioni di euro più bonus per ingaggiare Benjamin Sesko dal Lipsia.

C'è da dire che il Manchester United, in tempi recenti, si sia ritrovato con la coperta troppo corta per affrontare gli impegni stagionali: causa infortuni, malattie. squalifiche, ad esempio i Red Devils si sono sciolti in casa del Como senza ricambi in difesa e con De Ligt tornato titolare 325 giorni dopo l'ultima volta. Gran parte della colpa viene attribuita ai giocatori dal rendimento insufficiente - delusione totale anche con il Bournemouth di recente - ma rimane anche la sensazione che i risultati avrebbero dovuto essere migliori. L'assenza di Casemiro si è fatta sentire, per doti di leadership, qualità e palmares invidiabile. Anche la decisione forte di lasciare Bruno Fernandes in panchina contro le Cherries in Premier League avrebbe generato un certo malumore all'interno dello spogliatoio. Chi resterà a lavorare allo United, intanto, nutre grandi perplessità in termini di mentalità e attitudine dell'intera squadra che - al contrario di Carrick - non è stata esonerata.

Michael Fassbender’s Gritty New Sci-Fi Action Epic Is Officially Returning In 1 Month

📅 2026-11-12  |  Read Source →

The 2026 sci-fi movie, which is set to return soon, is the first directorial feature from South Korean filmmaker Na Hong-jin since his well-regarded 2016 horror movie The Wailing. The movie, which takes place in the isolated South Korean town of Hope Harbor and follows the human population fighting back against an alien invasion, features a star-studded ensemble cast that includes Hwang Jung-min (The Wailing), Jung Ho-yeon (Squid Game), Alicia Vikander (Ex Machina), and two-time nominee Michael Fassbender (X-Men: Days of Future Past). Hope reviews have been glowing, earning it a Certified Fresh 80% on Rotten Tomatoes.

Per Blu-ray.com, Hope is officially set to debut on Blu-ray via Decal Releasing on December 22. Amazon's official listing also reflects this date, putting the movie at an expected price point of $37.98. This release date sees the movie arriving on Blu-ray just seven months after its world premiere at the 79th annual Cannes Film Festival on May 17 and three months after its U.S. premiere on September 9.

Decal is the distribution company behind the physical media releases of many major recent independent movies and Oscar contenders, including Anatomy of a Fall, How to Blow Up a Pipeline, I, Tonya, Immaculate, It Was Just an Accident, Spencer, Triangle of Sadness, Longlegs, and the Best Picture winner Anora. They often handle the physical media for movies that were picked up for U.S. distribution by NEON, the company that handled Hope's domestic theatrical run.

In the U.S., NEON distributed both Anora and the 2019 Best Picture winner Parasite.

This physical media release could ultimately place Hope on sturdier ground, offering it a new avenue through which it can turn a profit. Ultimately, its break-even point is entirely unknown. However, it is reportedly the most expensive Korean production of all time. Its initial reported budget of $46 million, which Na Hong-jin said was inaccurate and too high (per NAVER Entertainment), could have placed its break-even point as high as $115 million.

While it is unclear how much lower than $46 million its budget truly is, the movie used a great deal of CGI effects to bring its aliens to life and features performances from an Oscar winner (Vikander won Best Supporting Actress in 2016 for The Danish Girl) and an Oscar nominee, so it probably did cost a somewhat substantial amount. Considering the fact that it has only earned $52.6 million worldwide so far, it is entirely possible that it is operating at a deficit and still needs to earn more aftermarket revenue in order to end up in the black.

Everton Remembrance Service Held At Goodison Park

📅 2026-11-12  |  Read Source →

Representatives from the Armed Forces, Everton Football Club and Everton fan groups gathered together at Goodison Park on Wednesday morning for the annual Everton Remembrance Day Service.

Organised by the Football Club and the Everton FC Heritage Society, the ceremony was led by Reverend Henry Corbett, the Chaplain to Finch Farm, and Father Paul Harris.

Club Ambassadors Ian Snodin and Graham Stuart laid wreaths on behalf of the Football Club and the Everton Former Players' Foundation, who were also represented by former Blues winger Ronny Goodlass.

The British Army, Royal Air Force, Royal Navy and Merchant Navy also laid floral tributes, as did the Everton Shareholders' Association, the Everton Former Players’ Foundation, Everton in Community and Everton Free School.

Everton in the Community’s veterans programme, the Everton Fan Advisory Board, the Tri-Service Toffees, the Everton Collection, Everton Football College and the Ruleteros Society were also represented.

Heritage Society member, Paul Kelly, delivered a short address, while a representative from Everton in the Community's veterans programme read the names of the fallen from the Everton Football Clubs of Merseyside, Chile and New Zealand. Bugler Sam Lovatt played the Last Post at 11am, preceding a two-minute period of silence.

Remembrance Day is commemorated every year on 11 November to mark the armistice signed between the Allies of the First World War and Germany at Compiègne, France, for the cessation of hostilities which took effect at 11am – the ‘11th hour of the 11th day of the 11th month’ – in 1918.

Those remembered from the three football clubs were:

First World War: Edward Atkinson, Frank Boundy, Harold Boyne, William Braidwood, James Brannick, Thomas Brewer, David Campbell, Arnold Cantell, Jeffrey Carmichael, Henry Churchill, Frederick Collinson, Alfred Corlett, Malcolm Fraser, Thomas Gracie, David Murray, Harry Norris, Thomas Norse, Leigh Richmond Roose, James Roy, Donald Sloan, Wilfred Toman, Richard Wynne.

Second World War: Brian Atkins, Alfred Pennington, William Reid, Thomas Robson, William Sumner.

Momiki Hat-Trick Sees Blues Thrash Durham

📅 2026-11-12  |  Read Source →

Yuka Momiki’s second-half hat-trick helped Everton make it four wins from four in the Subway Players Cup with an impressive 6-0 win against Durham.

The 30-year-old came on at half-time and netted a fine trio of goals, with a brace from Noemie Mouchon and a first goal for Everton for Jutta Rantala completing the scoring for the dominant Toffees.

The Blues set the tone for the evening as Mouchon rifled an effort in to the palms of Cat Sheppard within the opening minute.

Rantala then followed that up with a venomous strike of her own two minutes later as the keeper was called in to action once more.

The Finland international, who celebrated her birthday on Tuesday, came even closer on 9 minutes as her rising shot rattled the crossbar.

It was Rantala who then opened the scoring on 20 minutes as her persistence paid off. Hannah Cain pulled an enticing ball across the face of goal. Fellow forward Rantala brought it down before unleashing an unstoppable effort in to the top right corner to net her first goal in royal blue.

The hosts continued to control the tempo throughout the opening 45 – probing their opponents and boasting the clear majority of possession and chances.

At the break, Phelan made a double change as Rosa van Gool, Maz Pacheco and Momiki replaced Wheeler, Kitagawa and Vignola.

Those substitutes immediately paid off as Momiki fired home at the Gwladys Street End five minutes after the interval. Upon reaching the penalty spot, Everton’s number 29 elegantly placed the ball in to the bottom right corner.

That corner was found by the ball again on 64 minutes as Mouchon finished past the diving keeper. The French forward brought the ball down excellently – it almost sticking to her right foot. She then pulled the trigger early, slotting past the outrushed Sheppard.

One minute later, Plano came on for her Everton debut to mark another memorable evening for the Club’s Academy.

Momiki then doubled her tally in the 70th minute with the best of her three strikes. From the edge of the box, she curled in to the far corner. The Gwladys rose, with Momiki’s appreciation for the iconic stand reciprocated by the Evertonians’ cheers.

With five minutes remaining, Mouchon secured a brace of her own. The forward saw an initial strike saved, but prodded home the rebound to cap off a rapid break.

The loudest cheer of the night occurred only a single minute later as Momiki secured the match ball to conclude a brilliant evening to be a Blue.

My Everton #179: Loyalty & Love From Namibia

📅 2026-11-12  |  Read Source →

My love for Everton began when I was just eight years old.

At that age, football was more than just a game to me; it was something that brought me happiness, excitement and dreams.

Wayne Rooney was my favourite player and my biggest inspiration. He was not just a footballer I admired; he was someone I looked up to. In fact, when we played football in my village, my friends would call me "Rooney" because of how much I admired him and wanted to play like him.

When Rooney eventually moved to Manchester United, I found myself facing a difficult decision. Naturally, I wanted to follow him because he was my role model.

I thought about becoming a Manchester United supporter, but something inside me would not allow it. No matter how much I admired Rooney, my heart still belonged to Everton.

I realised my connection with the Club was much deeper than supporting a single player. It was a love that had already taken root in my heart.

Then came another special moment in my Everton journey. When Jordan Pickford joined the club in 2017, I was also transitioning into playing as a goalkeeper.

Watching him defend the goal inspired me in a new way. I saw in him someone I could learn from, someone who motivated me to become a better goalkeeper and strengthened my love for Everton.

From that moment, I became even more certain that Everton was my club, and I was proud to call myself a true Evertonian.

Being an Everton supporter in Namibia is not always easy.

We are few, and sometimes it feels like we are a rare breed. As far as I know, I have only met two other Everton supporters here, including my girlfriend, who is also an Evertonian.

Most of my friends often ask: "Bro, why do you support Everton?"

But my answer has always been simple: I support Everton because my love for this club is all about loyalty, passion, and the memories that have shaped who I am.

For me, Everton is more than just a football club. It is part of my childhood, part of my identity, and part of my story. From an eight-year-old boy who admired Wayne Rooney to the young man I am today, my love for this club has remained unchanged.

Every match, every goal, every victory, and even every heartbreak has added another chapter to my journey as an Evertonian.

I may be thousands of kilometres away, but my heart has always been with Everton.

Geography cannot weaken a love that has lasted for more than a decade, and difficult seasons cannot erase the memories that made me fall in love with this beautiful club.

I was there in the good times, I've been here in the difficult times and I will be here when the glory returns. Because once Everton gets into your heart, it never truly leaves.
