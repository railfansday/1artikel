What Martin O'Neill told Kasper Hogh about Celtic goal drought after Besiktas

📅 2026-11-07  |  Read Source →

As German referee Sascha Stegemann sounded his full-time whistle, Martin O'Neill raised his right fist to the smoke-filled Glasgow sky.

Celtic's Europa League campaign was finally up and running courtesy of a frenetic 3-2 victory over Besiktas, the manager visibly relieved and triumphant in equal measure.

O'Neill usually heads straight up the tunnel after matches. Thursday evening was different. He instead made a beeline for his players before their customary walk round on the Celtic Park turf, embracing captain Callum McGregor and goalscorers Callum Osmand and Camilo Duran.

A turning point in Celtic's season? It's too early to say. But O'Neill knew for certain that failure to beat Besiktas would have rendered the remainder of Celtic's Europa League campaign meaningless. Now they have fresh hope and a platform for progress.

O'Neill insists they must build on their gutsy European victory.

"That is what we want to do, absolutely," O'Neill said. "From the European side, we have given ourselves a chance. We will have to go and win away from home and it will not be easy."

Thursday evening saw the manager make five changes to his lineup. Jordan Lotomba recorded his first start at right-back and Kasper Hogh, Sebastian Tounekti, and James Forrest made up a second string frontline.

From Forrest, O'Neill wanted experience and added defensive security. Hogh offered a focal point in attack and went off to a standing ovation in the 77th minute.

It marked his 15th Celtic appearance without a goal but the home crowd were appreciative of his work rate, as was O'Neill.

"I want centre-forwards to be able to hold it and that is what he did," O'Neill stated. "He is not Duran, he is not a dribbler. But if he can do this for us against strong opposition it will stand him in good stead.

"I think Thursday night will be good for him. I said to him that I’m not worried about whether he scores a goal.

"Of course you want centre-forwards to score and of course centre-forwards live on them. But I said to him that last night I wanted him to be strong and fight when the ball is played up to him.

"I thought in terms of holding the ball up and bringing people into the game that he did really well."

Hogh, signed from Bodo/Glimt for a club record transfer fee in excess of £11 million during the summer transfer window, had no opportunities against Besiktas.

The Dane registered just one touch in the opposition penalty area and didn't have a single shot on goal.

Hogh is being starved of the service upon which he thrived at Bodo.

To that concern, O'Neill responded: "If they are going man for man and we have gone really, really low in terms of going close to the goalkeeper then the next one in if you are dinking it in is where a centre-forward has to play.

"If it is a decent ball in then he has to hold it up because he should be first. If it is in the air and he has to fight for it then it is difficult. But if you dink it in for him then you can start from there."

"Centre-forwards need a goal," O'Neill continued. "But that performance there against European opposition will give him confidence. I don’t know what side we will play on Sunday. But this lad has scored goals in the Champions League remember.

"He will get a confidence boost from Thursday night in terms of doing that part of the game well. But it will be nice for him to score a goal."

Hogh needn't have to worry about goals if Duran maintains his apex form. The Colombian couldn't have been more influential after coming off the bench against Besiktas, netting a crucial brace to give Celtic all three points.

First, Duran latched on to Osmand's reverse pass, rounding opposition goalkeeper Alexander Nubel and slotting into an empty net. He then brilliantly controlled Kieran Tierney's wayward shot, this time beating Nubel from six yards out.

"At this minute, he is on fire," O'Neill said of Duran. "He can play wide right, he has done that. He has played wide left for his other club. But centre-forward is where he is dynamic."

When Duran plays through the middle, Hogh is often sacrificed. Conversely, Hogh starting usually sees Duran shifted to the right flank. For O'Neill, it's onerous finding the perfect balance.

"We made five changes on Thursday night because it was necessary," he explained. "The boys who came on were always going to come on after 60 minutes.

"You can’t just plan things like this. Somebody might pick up an injury. Things change during the course of the game. But the plan before it was for these lads to go on. We were always going to make the changes.

"It was necessary if we wanted a bit more gusto in the side and a bit more playing in behind. That gave the opportunity for Duran to weave a bit of magic."

Focus rightly lay on Celtic's attacking play after Thursday. Yet their defence was equally pivotal, limiting Besiktas to few opportunities of note.

Beyond a penalty and the resulting rebound, from which they scored, Vincenzo Italiano's side offered little in the way of forward penetration, struggling to break down Celtic's organised structure. It took a long-range effort from Orkun Kokcu to score their second of the evening.

"It was really pleasing and really important," O'Neill said of his team's defending. "We conceded some ground in the game which was to be expected, but I thought defensively we were really, really strong.

"When corners came in we dealt with them and we dealt with second balls as well after it was played out. The back four was terrific. Lotomba did well until he conceded the penalty."

Central defender Cameron Carter-Vickers had one of his better displays. The American made nine clearances and won all but one of his five duels. Accurate on the ball too, Carter-Vickers brought an air of stability to the backline.

He recovered ably from his performance against Falkirk last weekend, when his meek clearance led directly to Ethan Ross' opener. Carter-Vickers came off in the latter stages with a hamstring complaint, but O'Neill thinks he had a point to prove.

"It looked that way," said the Celtic boss. "I thought he was very strong. He was complaining about his hamstrings being very tight at half-time and I thought we would give him another 15 minutes.

"Of course, the game changed in that time and we were chasing the game from behind. But the lads who came on in the forward positions were always going to come on. James Forrest was never going to last the whole game.

"I thought he was outstanding, an absolute credit to the football club with what he did for us. Even defensively. Everybody worked so hard. It was really pleasing."

The key for Celtic and O'Neill is building on Thursday's thrilling victory. As the manager knows all too well, it'll count for nothing if Kilmarnock, St Mirren, and Torreense aren't beaten in the coming weeks.

📅 2026-11-07  |  Read Source →

Thierno Barry scored two and created the other as Everton made it three wins on the spin to beat Coventry 3-1 in David Moyes' record-equalling match as Blues manager.

The Toffees had to recover from Yann Gboho's 52nd-minute opener as Barry headed home Vitalii Mykolenko's cross four minutes later, before taking his goal tally to 10 for  the season when he bundled in Tyrique George's centre just past the hour mark.

Three points – which lifted Everton into fifth place in the Premier League table ahead of this weekend's round of matches – were then secured against Frank Lampard's top-flight newcomers nine minutes from time when Barry slid in Kiernan Dewsbury-Hall to blast past Carl Rushworth.

That ensured the Blues went into the upcoming international break buoyant as Moyes tasted victory in his 592nd match in charge of the Club, levelling the previous record set by Harry Catterick.

Moyes made one change from the team that triumphed 1-0 at Newcastle four days earlier, Tyler Dibling getting the nod at right wing as Merlin Röhl dropped to right-back in place of Jake O'Brien.

After a bright opening from the visitors, Everton soon found a foothold in the game to carve out three opportunities in quick succession. A slick one-two between George and Dewsbury-Hall on nine minutes – that started with a lovely backheel from the winger – allowed George to play Barry through on the right side of the penalty area. The Frenchman did not get the ball set with his first touch, however, as he was pushed wide and unable to get a clean strike on goal.

Seconds later, Coventry made a hash of a defensive clearance, allowing Mykolenko – still up from the previous attack – a sight at goal as he drove into the arms of keeper Carl Rushworth from just inside the area.

The best chance so far soon followed as Branthwaite won a recycled header from Dewsbury-Hall's free-kick, leading to a scramble in the box involving Barry and Armstrong that ended with the Academy graduate trying to nod home his maiden Blues goal before Coventry cleared.

Everton couldn't build on that momentum, though, and Coventry were the next to threaten as the first half wore on, Pickford needing to save Sakamoto's shot with his legs after the Japanese winger had been slid through, before Jack Rudoni whistled a low angled drive inches beyond the left-hand post.

The Blues could count themselves fortunate not to go in behind at half-time but it wasn't long before Coventry were in front after the restart.

Lampard's side were allowed too much space to move in from the right channel as Gboho played a one-two with Sakamoto and knocked a close-range effort through Branthwaite's legs and past Pickford.

It left Everton needing a strong response – and they duly delivered with a quickfire double from that man Barry.

Just four minutes after Coventry's opener, the striker got on the end of Mykolenko's pinpoint left-wing cross to nod into the bottom right-hand corner.

And Barry made it double figures for the season just after the hour mark as George turned provider from the left flank this time, floating a ball to the back post for the Frenchman to somehow squeeze over the line off his midriff.

Barry may have been making headlines for his goalscoring this season but he showed the quality in his all-round game to make a fine Everton turnaround complete with nine minutes to go.

Collecting possession just inside Coventry's half, he spun and threaded a beauty of a through ball down the left channel for Dewsbury-Hall to chase. Former Australia opener Usman Khawaja has claimed that the side's entire dressing room knew about the ball-tampering incident in Cape Town in 2018.

Khawaja, who retired from international duty after Australia's 4-1 Ashes thrashing of England last winter, said: "It was all of us. Not the batters, not the bowlers, not the staff. All of us."

Captain-at-the-time Smith, Bancroft and Warner, who was found to have been the architect of the 'Sandpapergate' plot, all received bans after the incident, with Smith and Warner suspended for one year and Bancroft handed a nine-month sanction.

Bowlers Pat Cummins, Mitchell Starc, Josh Hazlewood and Nathan Lyon issued a joint statement in 2021 insisting they had no idea about the ball-tampering.

That came after Bancroft - who had aimed to conceal the sandpaper down his trousers during the Test in question - replied "I think it's probably self-explanatory" when asked in a newspaper interview whether the bowling attack knew about the scandal.

Cummins, Starc and Hazlewood have all been rested from this month's white-ball series at home to England while Lyon is not in the limited-overs set-up, so the quartet may not be quizzed on Khawaja's claims just yet.

Khawaja wrote in his book that ball-tampering plans were first discussed in the second Test of the 2018 series in Port Elizabeth, now Gqeberha.

He continued: "In an ideal world, I don't want to do this.

"I don't want to be the one who comes out and talks about the truth. The issue is that I genuinely thought after eight years the truth would have been out by now.

"Maybe I thought Davey [Warner] would have exposed it, or someone else would have talked about it more.

"There has been a great deal of speculation in the eight years since the 2018 Cape Town Test, including extensive media coverage throughout last month's series in South Africa.

"During this time there has been the ongoing opportunity to present any relevant information, and this remains the case.

"All relevant information presented to CA to date has been properly considered and we remain comfortable with the findings of our integrity process."

"At the end of the day, I think it's the right thing to do, particularly for those three guys. And I think most of the guys in the team, at some level, will be relieved we can finally move on.

"Even in the change rooms when I was talking to them after the game. I said: 'Take this as a team. We did this as a team. We knew what was going on. We have to take it as a team'.

"I was always of that stance. Obviously, it didn't work that way, and those three guys got crucified.

"I've seen Davey over the last eight years just get nailed bad over and over again. It's taken a big man not to say anything and just to stay quiet. He's just copped it and copped it and copped it.

Circulating concentrations of low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) and triglyceride-rich lipoproteins are leading risk factors for atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease1. Despite several approved lipid-lowering medications, most patients fail to achieve target lipid levels under the chronic care treatment model2,3. Here, we describe a potential single-course treatment in which an infusion of guide RNA and mRNA encoding a CRISPR adenine base editor—encapsulated within a GalNAc–lipid nanoparticle—leads to durable, liver-selective inactivation of angiopoietin-like 3 (ANGPTL3), a known driver of increased LDL-C and triglyceride concentrations. Analyses in human liver cells in vitro confirmed that the base editor introduces a DNA base-pair change at the target ANGPTL3 locus with no detectable off-target editing at any of approximately 3000 candidate sites or treatment-related structural variants. Targeted ANGPTL3 inactivation in nonhuman primates was well-tolerated and led to a > 90% mean reduction in circulating ANGPTL3 protein, with durability confirmed out to 3 years following a single dose. In mouse and nonhuman primate models of hyperlipidemia, gene inactivation was associated with reductions of approximately 40 to 50% in LDL-C and 50 to 70% in triglycerides. These results provided the scientific foundation for initiation of an ongoing first-in-human clinical trial in patients with refractory hyperlipidemia syndromes.

Paraguay's Senate on Friday impeached President Santiago Pena over a banking crisis that left millions of people unable to access their deposits.

The move cleared the way for Vice President Pedro Alliana to succeed him. He will serve the rest of the presidential term, which ends in August 2028.

Pena had already resigned on Thursday, but by impeaching and convicting him instead of accepting his resignation, senators stripped him of the lifetime Senate seat and immunity granted to former presidents, leaving him vulnerable to prosecution.

Following the Senate vote, prosecutors ordered the arrest of the ousted president alongside former central bank chief Carlos Carvallo as part of a criminal investigation.

Officials later said Pena was placed under house arrest pending a decision by the public prosecutor's office on the final charges against him.

The bank's accounts — also used to process pension payments — are frozen, leaving millions of customers unable to access their money. Ueno also held hundreds of millions of dollars in public funds.

Pena's resignation on Thursday followed the withdrawal of support by Congress President Basilio Nunez and ruling conservative Colorado Party leader Horacio Cartes.

Thirty-five opposition lawmakers and ruling-party dissidents had submitted impeachment articles in the 80-seat Chamber of Deputies, accusing Pena of conflicts of interest, misuse of pension funds and institutional cover-ups linked to Ueno.

Ueno served about 2.94 million customers, roughly 60% of Paraguay's adult population.

As of last month, the bank said it held roughly $800 million in public-sector deposits — about a quarter of its total deposits — including money from the operators of Paraguay's giant hydroelectric dams.

Paraguay's central bank ordered a resolution or closure process Tuesday after finding that Ueno's solvency ratio had fallen below half the required minimum.

Ueno's app, card payments and transfers stopped working, while branches closed, leaving customers queuing outside unable to withdraw their savings.

Hundreds of employees of Ueno and other companies in its parent conglomerate protested outside the Labor Ministry Thursday, demanding unpaid wages and severance.

Retirees, depositors, students and workers had marched through Asuncion a day earlier, demanding Pena's impeachment and holding signs saying "Give us back our money."

Authorities detained eight senior executives, including founder Miguel Vazquez and bank president Juan Manuel Gustale. Prosecutors also charged 27 bank officials and business associates with offenses including breach of trust and attempted fraud.

Lawyers for several detainees said they needed time to review the allegations before commenting.

Pena had sought to reassure customers in a video message Wednesday, pledging legislation "that will return to every saver what they are entitled to as quickly as possible."

The crisis also forced him to cancel his attendance at the Ibero-American summit in Madrid.

Pena, a conservative economist, took office in August 2023 for a five-year term.
